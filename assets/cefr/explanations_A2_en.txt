1.1 "Last weekend" — "Last" + time word = the one before now. No preposition: not "in last weekend".
1.2 "we visited" — Past Simple of a regular verb: visit + -ed.
1.3 "my grandparents" — One plural word for grandmother and grandfather together.
1.4 "They live" — Present Simple, because this is still true today.
1.5 "in a small village" — "In" for villages and towns. "A" because the listener does not know it.
1.6 "near the sea" — "The sea" always takes "the".
1.7 "We stayed" — Past Simple again: stay + -ed.
1.8 "there" — "There" = in the village. Never say "to there".
1.9 "until Sunday evening" — "Until" = up to that moment and not later.
2.1 "I started" — Past Simple with -ed for a finished action.
2.2 "a new job" — "Job" is countable, so it needs "a".
2.3 "in March" — "In" + months, seasons and years.
2.4 "The office" — "The" because it is the office of my new job; we both know which one.
2.5 "is much bigger" — Comparative: big → bigger (we double the "g"). "Much" makes the difference stronger.
2.6 "than my old one" — After a comparative we always use "than". "One" = office.
2.7 "I like" — Present Simple for an opinion or a feeling.
2.8 "my team" — A group of people. In this sentence it takes a singular verb.
2.9 ", but the hours are long" — "But" shows contrast. "The hours" = the working hours.
3.1 "Yesterday" — A past time word. It can go at the beginning or at the end.
3.2 "I forgot my umbrella" — Irregular verb: forget → forgot.
3.3 "at home" — Fixed phrase with no article.
3.4 "It rained" — Weather sentences need the subject "it". Here "rain" is a verb.
3.5 "all afternoon" — "All + time" = from the beginning to the end of it.
3.6 ", so I got very wet" — "Get + adjective" = become. Past form: get → got.
3.7 "Next time" — About the future. No preposition with "next".
3.8 "I will check" — "Will" for a decision you make at the moment of speaking.
3.9 "the weather first" — "The weather" always has "the". "First" = before going out.
4.1 "We are going" — Present Continuous for a fixed future plan.
4.2 "to Portugal" — "To" shows direction. Most countries take no article.
4.3 "in July" — "In" + month, the same rule as in line 2.
4.4 "My wife" — Possessive word instead of an article.
4.5 "booked the tickets" — Past Simple. "Book" = buy a place in advance.
4.6 "last week" — "Last" + time, again with no preposition.
4.7 "I still need" — "Still" = the situation has not changed yet.
4.8 "to buy" — After "need" we use "to + verb".
4.9 "a new suitcase" — "A" because it is one of many, not a known one.
5.1 "My phone" — Possessive "my", no article with it.
5.2 "broke" — Irregular: break → broke. Here nobody broke it; it stopped working.
5.3 "last Tuesday" — "Last" + day of the week, no preposition.
5.4 "I took it" — Irregular: take → took. "It" = the phone.
5.5 "to a small shop" — "To" for the direction of movement.
5.6 "near my office" — "Near" needs no "to" after it.
5.7 "The repair" — "The" because it is the repair of my phone.
5.8 "was cheap" — Past of "is". "Cheap" = it did not cost much money.
5.9 ", but it took three days" — "Take + time" = need that much time.
6.1 "I usually" — Adverbs of frequency go between the subject and the verb.
6.2 "cook" — Present Simple, no "-s" because the subject is "I".
6.3 "at home" — Fixed phrase with no article, as in line 3.
6.4 "Restaurants" — Plural with no article = restaurants in general.
6.5 "are nice" — A plural subject takes "are".
6.6 ", but they are expensive" — "But" contrasts the good side and the bad side.
6.7 "Last month" — Past time expression, no preposition.
6.8 "I ate out" — Irregular: eat → ate. "Eat out" = eat in a restaurant.
6.9 "only twice" — "Twice" = two times. "Only" says that this is a small number.
7.1 "My manager" — The person who is your boss at work.
7.2 "asked me" — Pattern: ask + somebody + to do something. The person comes first.
7.3 "to write a report" — "To + verb" after "ask somebody".
7.4 "I finished" — Past Simple with -ed.
7.5 "the first part" — We use "the" with ordinal numbers: the first, the second.
7.6 "yesterday evening" — Two time words together: which day + which part of the day.
7.7 "I am going to send" — "Going to" for a plan you already have.
7.8 "the rest" — "The rest" = everything that is left.
7.9 "on Friday" — "On" + day of the week.
8.1 "When I was a child" — "When" + past = at that time in my life.
8.2 "I hated" — Past Simple of a strong feeling: hate + -d.
8.3 "vegetables" — Plural with no article = vegetables in general.
8.4 "Now" — It shows the contrast with the past.
8.5 "I eat them" — "Them" = the vegetables. The object goes after the verb.
8.6 "almost every day" — "Almost" = nearly, but not one hundred per cent.
8.7 "People" — Irregular plural of "person". It always takes a plural verb.
8.8 "can change" — After "can" the verb has no "to" and no "-s".
8.9 ", I think" — A soft ending: it turns the sentence into an opinion, not a fact.
9.1 "The gym" — "The" because it is one particular gym.
9.2 "near my house" — This part tells us which gym: the one near my house.
9.3 "closed last month" — Past Simple. Here "close" = stop working for ever.
9.4 "Now" — It marks the change from the old situation.
9.5 "I run" — Present Simple for a new habit.
9.6 "in the park" — "In" + place; "the" because it is my usual park.
9.7 "It is free" — "Free" here means "you pay nothing".
9.8 ", and the air" — "Air" is uncountable; "the" because it is the air in this park.
9.9 "is much better" — Irregular comparative: good → better. "Much" makes it stronger.
10.1 "I bought" — Irregular: buy → bought.
10.2 "a bike" — "Bike" is the normal short word for "bicycle".
10.3 "in the spring" — "In" + season. "In spring" without "the" is also correct.
10.4 "I use it" — "It" = the bike, so we do not repeat the noun.
10.5 "almost" — It makes "every day" a little weaker: nearly every day.
10.6 "every day" — Frequency, two words. "Everyday" as one word is an adjective and means "ordinary".
10.7 "It is faster" — Comparative of a short adjective: fast → faster.
10.8 "than the bus" — "Than" comes after every comparative.
10.9 ", and I feel better" — "Feel" + adjective. "Better" is the comparative of "well".
