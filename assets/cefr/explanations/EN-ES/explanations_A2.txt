1.1 «El fin de semana pasado» — Spanish keeps the article and puts the adjective last: "el fin de semana pasado", literally "the weekend past". English "last weekend" has neither article nor preposition, so both parts have to be added.
1.2 «visitamos» — Preterite of "visitar", used for a finished action at a definite time. In the "-ar" verbs the "we" form is the same in the present and the preterite, so only the context tells them apart.
1.3 «a mis abuelos» — Spanish marks a human direct object with the personal "a": "visitamos a mis abuelos". Leaving it out is a real mistake, not just a stylistic slip. "Abuelos" in the plural covers both grandparents, as English "grandparents" does.
1.4 «Viven» — The subject is left out, because "viven" already says who. Spanish uses the simple present for a permanent situation, exactly like English.
1.5 «en un pueblo pequeño» — "Un" because the village is new to the conversation, and the adjective goes after the noun: "un pueblo pequeño", the reverse of "a small village".
1.6 «cerca del mar» — "Cerca" needs "de", and "de" plus "el" always contract into "del": "cerca del mar". Writing "de el" is never correct.
1.7 «Nos quedamos» — "Quedarse" is reflexive, so the pronoun "nos" comes before the verb. The preterite marks the stay as finished.
1.8 «allí» — "Allí" means "there" and takes no preposition after a verb. Spanish also has "ahí" for something a bit nearer, but "allí" is the natural choice here.
1.9 «hasta el domingo por la tarde» — "Hasta" means "until". The day keeps its article, "el domingo", and the part of the day is added with "por": "por la tarde". English needs neither.
2.1 «Empecé» — Preterite of "empezar", first person. Note the spelling change: "empecé" with "c", because "z" becomes "c" before "e". The accent marks the stressed final syllable.
2.2 «un trabajo nuevo» — "Un" because the job is mentioned for the first time, and the adjective follows the noun: "un trabajo nuevo". Placing "nuevo" before it would suggest "another" rather than "brand new".
2.3 «en marzo» — Months take "en": "en marzo", "en julio". They are written in lower case in Spanish, unlike English.
2.4 «La oficina» — "La" because it is a particular office, the one at the new job. The noun is feminine, so the article is "la".
2.5 «es mucho más grande» — Comparatives are built with "más": "más grande". "Mucho" intensifies the comparison, and "ser" is used because this is a lasting property.
2.6 «que la anterior» — After a comparison comes "que". "La anterior" is an adjective used on its own, agreeing in the feminine with "oficina": Spanish needs no equivalent of English "one".
2.7 «Me gusta» — "Gustar" works backwards: the team is the subject and the person is the indirect object, "me". Literally "my team is pleasing to me", which is why there is no word for "I".
2.8 «mi equipo» — Because it is the grammatical subject of "gusta", "mi equipo" is in its plain form, and the verb is singular to agree with it.
2.9 «pero la jornada es larga» — "Jornada" means the working day, not the day in general — that would be "día". "Ser" is used for a lasting characteristic, and "larga" agrees with the feminine noun.
3.1 «Ayer» — "Ayer" is an adverb: no preposition, no article. It usually opens the sentence, as in English.
3.2 «olvidé el paraguas» — Spanish prefers the article where English uses a possessive: "el paraguas", not "mi paraguas", when it is obviously yours. "Olvidé" is the preterite of "olvidar".
3.3 «en casa» — "En casa" is a fixed phrase with no article. "En la casa" would mean inside one particular house.
3.4 «Llovió» — Weather verbs have no subject at all in Spanish: "llovió" stands alone. English needs the empty "it", and Spanish has nothing to put in its place.
3.5 «toda la tarde» — "Toda la tarde" keeps the article, literally "all the afternoon". And Spanish "la tarde" covers both English "afternoon" and "evening" — here it is the afternoon.
3.6 «así que me mojé mucho» — "Así que" introduces the result. "Mojarse" is reflexive, hence "me", and "mucho" follows the verb rather than preceding it.
3.7 «La próxima vez» — "La próxima vez" keeps its article, where English says "next time" with nothing at all.
3.8 «voy a mirar» — "Ir a" plus the infinitive is the everyday future: "voy a mirar". The pronoun is left out, since "voy" already says who.
3.9 «el tiempo antes» — "El tiempo" means "the weather" as well as "time"; only context separates them. "Antes" at the end means "beforehand".
4.1 «Vamos a ir» — "Ir a" plus the infinitive again, this time with "ir" itself as the main verb: "vamos a ir". Spanish tolerates the repetition without any awkwardness.
4.2 «a Portugal» — Direction is marked with "a": "ir a Portugal". Most country names take no article in Spanish.
4.3 «en julio» — Months with "en": "en julio", the same rule as "en marzo" above.
4.4 «Mi mujer» — "Mi mujer" is the ordinary way to say "my wife" in Spain; "mi esposa" is more formal. "Mi" does not change for gender.
4.5 «compró los billetes» — Preterite of "comprar". "Los billetes" keeps the article because these are the tickets for this trip. In Latin America the word would be "los boletos".
4.6 «la semana pasada» — "La semana pasada" keeps the article and takes no preposition — the adjective after the noun does the work of English "last".
4.7 «Todavía necesito» — "Todavía" means "still" and stands before the verb. "Necesitar" is used in the present because it describes a current state.
4.8 «comprar» — After "necesitar" the second verb goes straight into the infinitive with no preposition: "necesito comprar". English inserts "to"; Spanish inserts nothing.
4.9 «una maleta nueva» — "Una" because the suitcase is new to the conversation, and the adjective follows: "una maleta nueva", agreeing in the feminine.
5.1 «Mi teléfono» — "Mi" replaces the article. It has one form for both genders, and only the noun tells you that "teléfono" is masculine.
5.2 «se rompió» — "Romperse" is reflexive here: the phone broke by itself. Without "se" somebody would have broken it, and Spanish makes that distinction with the pronoun where English changes nothing.
5.3 «el martes pasado» — Days of the week take the article and no preposition: "el martes pasado". Adding "en" would be a mistake.
5.4 «Lo llevé» — "Lo" is the masculine direct object pronoun, standing for the phone, and it goes before the conjugated verb: "lo llevé", never "llevé lo".
5.5 «a un taller pequeño» — "A" marks the destination after "llevar". "Un" because the repair shop is new to the conversation, and "pequeño" follows the noun.
5.6 «cerca de mi oficina» — "Cerca de" needs both words. Here there is no contraction, because "de" is followed by "mi", not by "el".
5.7 «La reparación» — "La" because it is the repair of that particular phone, already mentioned.
5.8 «fue barata» — "Ser" in the preterite, "fue", for a completed characteristic. "Barata" agrees in the feminine with "reparación".
5.9 «pero tardó tres días» — "Tardar" means "to take time": "tardó tres días". The subject is still the repair, and it is left unsaid.
6.1 «Normalmente» — "Normalmente" is an adverb of frequency and opens the sentence comfortably. Adverbs in "-mente" are built from the feminine form of the adjective.
6.2 «cocino» — First person of "cocinar" in the present, for a habit. No pronoun is needed, and no auxiliary either.
6.3 «en casa» — "En casa" fixed and article-free, as in the third phrase.
6.4 «Los restaurantes» — The plural article is what makes it general: "los restaurantes" means restaurants as a whole. English drops the article for the same idea, which is why it goes missing here.
6.5 «están bien» — "Estar bien" is about a state or an opinion, whereas "ser bueno" would judge their quality permanently. That is the "ser" and "estar" split, and English has no equivalent.
6.6 «pero son caros» — Here "ser" is right, because being expensive is a lasting property: "son caros". The adjective agrees in the masculine plural with "restaurantes".
6.7 «El mes pasado» — "El mes pasado" keeps the article and takes no preposition, the same pattern as "la semana pasada".
6.8 «comí fuera» — "Comer fuera" means "to eat out". "Fuera" is an adverb and needs nothing after it.
6.9 «solo dos veces» — "Solo" means "only" and goes before what it limits. "Dos veces" is how Spanish says "twice" — there is no single word for it.
7.1 «Mi jefe» — "Mi jefe" with a possessive instead of an article. The word means "boss" and is used for any level of manager.
7.2 «me pidió» — "Pedir" takes an indirect object: "me pidió", literally "asked to me". This is why the pronoun is "me" and not "a mí" alone.
7.3 «escribir un informe» — After "pedir" Spanish can use the plain infinitive when the subject is obvious: "me pidió escribir". "Un informe" takes "un" because the report is new to the conversation.
7.4 «Terminé» — Preterite of "terminar" for a finished action. The accent falls on the last syllable and must be written.
7.5 «la primera parte» — Ordinals agree with the noun: "la primera parte", feminine to match "parte". The article is obligatory.
7.6 «ayer por la tarde» — "Ayer por la tarde" combines the adverb with "por la tarde" — the part of the day always needs "por" plus the article.
7.7 «Voy a enviar» — "Ir a" plus the infinitive for a planned future, the same construction as "voy a mirar" earlier.
7.8 «el resto» — "El resto" keeps the article, exactly like English "the rest". The word is masculine.
7.9 «el viernes» — Days of the week take the article and no preposition: "el viernes". With the plural article, "los viernes", it would mean "every Friday".
8.1 «Cuando era niño» — "Era" is the imperfect of "ser", used for a state that lasted. And Spanish drops the article before a noun of age or profession here: "era niño", not "era un niño".
8.2 «odiaba» — The imperfect again, for a feeling that went on over years: "odiaba". The preterite "odié" would point at one single moment.
8.3 «las verduras» — Spanish needs the article to speak generally: "las verduras". English says "vegetables" with nothing, and that missing "las" is the usual slip.
8.4 «Ahora» — "Ahora" is an adverb of time with no preposition. At the start of the sentence it sets the present against the past.
8.5 «las como» — "Las" is the feminine plural object pronoun, standing for "las verduras", and it goes before the verb: "las como".
8.6 «casi todos los días» — "Casi" stands before what it softens, and "todos los días" keeps its article: literally "almost all the days".
8.7 «La gente» — "La gente" is singular in Spanish even though it means many people: "la gente puede", never "la gente pueden". English "people" is plural, which makes this an easy mistake.
8.8 «puede cambiar» — After "poder" the verb goes into the infinitive with no preposition: "puede cambiar".
8.9 «creo» — "Creo" at the end softens the statement, like English "I think". The pronoun is left out and a comma separates it.
9.1 «El gimnasio» — "El" because it is one particular gym, identified by the words that follow.
9.2 «cerca de mi casa» — "Cerca de mi casa" modifies "gimnasio" and comes straight after it. There is no contraction here, because "de" is followed by "mi".
9.3 «cerró el mes pasado» — "Cerrar" is used without "se" when the closing is treated as an event in its own right. "El mes pasado" keeps the article and takes no preposition.
9.4 «Ahora» — "Ahora" opens the contrast with the past, as in the eighth phrase.
9.5 «corro» — First person of "correr" in the present, for a new habit.
9.6 «por el parque» — "Por el parque" means moving around inside it. "En el parque" would simply place you there, without the idea of running through it.
9.7 «Es gratis» — "Gratis" is invariable: it never changes for gender or number. "Ser" is used because it is a permanent property of the park.
9.8 «y el aire» — "El aire" keeps its article, as Spanish does with any noun used as a subject. The word is masculine despite ending in "-e".
9.9 «es mucho mejor» — "Mejor" is the irregular comparative of "bueno" and needs no "más" in front. "Mucho" intensifies it, exactly as in the second phrase.
10.1 «Compré» — Preterite of "comprar", first person, with a written accent on the last syllable: "compré".
10.2 «una bici» — "Una" because the bike is new to the conversation. "Bici" is the everyday short form of "bicicleta" and keeps the feminine gender.
10.3 «en primavera» — Seasons take "en", usually with no article: "en primavera", "en verano".
10.4 «La uso» — "La" is the feminine object pronoun, standing for "la bici", and it comes before the verb: "la uso".
10.5 «casi» — "Casi" goes before what it softens, exactly as in the eighth phrase.
10.6 «todos los días» — "Todos los días" keeps the article, literally "all the days". English "every day" has none.
10.7 «Es más rápida» — The comparative with "más": "más rápida", agreeing in the feminine with "bici". "Ser" is used for a lasting property.
10.8 «que el autobús» — After the comparison comes "que", and "el autobús" keeps its article — Spanish uses it where English says just "the bus" for the service in general.
10.9 «y me siento mejor» — "Sentirse" is reflexive, hence "me", and it is stem-changing: "me siento", "te sientes". "Mejor" is the same irregular comparative as in the ninth phrase.
