1.1 «La reorganización se anunció» — "Se anunció" is the impersonal "se", where English uses the passive "was announced". The doer is not named because it does not matter.
1.2 «un martes» — "Un martes" with the indefinite article means "one Tuesday, some Tuesday". With "el martes" it would be a specific one, and the whole joke depends on that "un".
1.3 «que es cuando nunca se anuncia nada bueno» — "Que es cuando" is a relative clause referring to the whole preceding idea. "Nunca" before the verb needs no extra "no", and "nada bueno" places the adjective after the pronoun.
1.4 «Nos prometieron» — "Nos prometieron" is a subjectless plural verb. Spanish cannot make the person the subject of a passive here, so the English "we were promised" has to be recast.
1.5 «menos niveles» — "Menos niveles" uses one word for both countable and uncountable, so the English split between "fewer" and "less" has no equivalent.
1.6 «y decisiones más rápidas» — "Decisiones más rápidas" builds the comparative with "más" and places it after the noun, along with the adjective. English puts both before.
1.7 «Seis meses después» — "Seis meses después" needs no preposition, and "después" follows the period.
1.8 «tenemos ambas cosas» — "Ambas cosas" is more formal than "las dos". Note that "ambas" needs no article, unlike "las dos".
1.9 «y además dos comités nuevos» — "Y además" adds with a slight shift. "Dos comités nuevos" puts the adjective after the noun; before it, "nuevos comités", it would mean "further ones".
1.10 «Mi propio puesto» — "Mi propio puesto" doubles the possessive for emphasis. "Propio" agrees with the noun and must follow the possessive, never replace it.
1.11 «no ha cambiado formalmente» — The present perfect for something whose result still holds. "Formalmente" means "officially", and the adverb comes after the participle.
1.12 «cosa que nadie termina de creerse» — "Cosa que" refers back to the whole clause, and it is a slightly more colloquial alternative to "lo cual". "Terminar de" plus the infinitive means "to quite do something", which is exactly the shade English gets from "quite".
1.13 «Ahora dependo» — "Depender de" always takes "de", and it is the standard verb for reporting to someone.
1.14 «de alguien» — "De alguien" carries the "de" demanded by the verb. "Alguien" is invariable.
1.15 «que depende de mi anterior jefa» — "Que depende de" repeats the verb and its preposition. "Mi anterior jefa" puts "anterior" before the noun, where it means "previous"; after it, the sense would be closer to "the one before in a list".
1.16 «Para ser justos» — "Para ser justos" is a fixed parenthetical phrase using the infinitive.
1.17 «ella es muy buena» — "Ella es muy buena" adds the pronoun for contrast, which Spanish does only deliberately. "Ser" is used because it is a lasting quality.
1.18 «y las reuniones son más cortas» — "Más cortas" agrees in the feminine plural with "reuniones". "Ser" again, because it is presented as how things now are.
1.19 «Si todo esto» — "Si" introduces a noun clause acting as the subject, not a condition. This "si" carries no accent.
1.20 «cuenta como menos niveles» — "Contar como" takes "as" in English and "como" here. The phrase repeats the wording from the second line, and the repetition is deliberate.
1.21 «depende de cómo se cuente» — "Depende de cómo se cuente" uses the subjunctive after "cómo" because the manner is left open. The accent on "cómo" survives inside the indirect question.
2.1 «Lo más difícil» — "Lo más difícil" uses the neuter article with a superlative, "the hardest thing". Spanish needs no word for "thing".
2.2 «de hablar bien un idioma extranjero» — "De hablar" carries the "de" demanded by "lo más difícil". "Bien" goes between the verb and its object, which Spanish allows and English does not.
2.3 «es que la gente deja de hacerte concesiones» — "Dejar de" plus the infinitive means "to stop doing". "Hacerte concesiones" attaches the pronoun to the infinitive.
2.4 «En el B1» — "En el B1" keeps the article before the level, unlike English "at B1".
2.5 «todos se emocionaban» — "Emocionarse de" is reflexive and takes "de" before the clause. "Todos" is plural and takes a plural verb, unlike English "everyone".
2.6 «de que supiera pedir un café» — "De que supiera" requires the subjunctive, because the emotion is a reaction rather than a report. "Un café" with the article means one cup, as opposed to uncountable coffee.
2.7 «En algún punto después» — "En algún punto después" uses "punto" for a point in time. "Algún" is the shortened form before a masculine noun.
2.8 «se acabaron los halagos» — "Se acabaron" is reflexive and puts the verb before the subject, which Spanish does to introduce something new.
2.9 «y empezaron las correcciones» — "Empezaron las correcciones" repeats that word order for symmetry. The parallel is what produces the effect.
2.10 «Eso es progreso» — "Eso es progreso" leaves "progreso" without an article, which Spanish does when the noun is a bare predicate.
2.11 «aunque rara vez lo parezca» — "Aunque ... lo parezca" takes the subjunctive because the concession is being conceded rather than stated. "Lo" is the neuter pronoun standing for the whole idea.
2.12 «en el momento» — "En el momento" keeps the article and means "at the time". Without it, "en momentos", the sense would change entirely.
2.13 «El último diez por ciento» — "El último diez por ciento" keeps the article before "último", and the verb that follows is singular.
2.14 «no es vocabulario» — "No es vocabulario" negates the copula and opens the "no ... sino ..." frame.
2.15 «sino saber cuándo no pasarse de listo» — "Sino" closes the frame and cannot be replaced by "pero". "Saber cuándo no pasarse de listo" chains an infinitive, an indirect question and a negated reflexive infinitive.
2.16 «Todavía me paso» — "Pasarse" is reflexive and means "to overdo it". "Todavía" goes before the verb.
2.17 «normalmente con una palabra» — "Con una palabra" uses "con" for the means.
2.18 «que aprendí esa misma mañana» — The relative "que" cannot be dropped, unlike English "that". "Esa misma mañana" places "misma" after the demonstrative for emphasis.
2.19 «La fluidez» — "La fluidez" keeps the article, as any subject noun does.
2.20 «por lo que voy viendo» — "Por lo que voy viendo" is a fixed parenthetical phrase using "ir" plus a gerund, which conveys a gradual process English would render with "as far as I can tell".
2.21 «es sobre todo contención» — "Contención" needs no article as a bare predicate. "Sobre todo" softens the claim.

3.3 «que en casa de la mayoría de mis amigos» — "En casa de" is the fixed way to say "at somebody's home", and Spanish has no possessive apostrophe to do it with. "La mayoría de" takes "de".
3.4 «Cada visita» — "Cada visita" takes a singular noun, as "cada" always does.
3.5 «exige un documento» — "Exigir" is stronger than "necesitar" and fits the bureaucratic tone.
3.6 «que solo existe después de la visita anterior» — "Solo" as an adverb takes no accent. "Después de" needs both words, and "la visita anterior" puts the adjective after the noun.
3.7 «La señora de la ventanilla cuatro» — "La señora de la ventanilla cuatro" links the nouns with "de", and the number follows the noun with no article, as in "la habitación cinco".
3.8 «ha desarrollado» — The present perfect "ha desarrollado", for something that has taken shape and still holds.
3.9 «cierta simpatía por mí» — "Cierta simpatía" without an article means "a certain amount of". With "una cierta" it would sound heavier and slightly odd here. "Simpatía por" takes "por".
3.10 «Ya no me pregunta» — "Ya no" together means "no longer". "Me pregunta" puts the pronoun before the verb.
3.11 «a qué vengo» — "A qué vengo" keeps the "a" demanded by "venir a", and the accent on "qué" survives in the indirect question.
3.12 «simplemente alarga la mano hacia el sello correcto» — The semicolon joins two independent clauses. "Alargar la mano hacia" is the natural phrase where English says "reach for".
3.13 «La semana pasada» — "La semana pasada" keeps the article and takes no preposition.
3.14 «me dijo» — "Me dijo" puts the indirect object pronoun before the verb.
3.15 «que ya casi había terminado» — "Ya casi había terminado" uses the pluperfect, because the finishing is set before the moment of speaking. "Ya casi" stacks two adverbs, which is idiomatic.
3.16 «Eso ya lo había oído antes» — "Eso ya lo había oído antes" doubles the object: "eso" is fronted and "lo" repeats it. Spanish requires that doubling whenever the object comes first.
3.17 «y he aprendido» — The present perfect again in "he aprendido", with the auxiliary repeated. Spanish repeats it more readily than English does.
3.18 «a no celebrarlo demasiado pronto» — "Aprender a" takes "a", and the negation goes before the infinitive: "a no celebrarlo".
3.19 «Aun así» — "Aun así" means "even so", and "aun" here carries no accent.
3.20 «hay algo curiosamente reconfortante» — "Algo curiosamente reconfortante" places the adjective after "algo", as Spanish always does with this pronoun.
3.21 «en una burocracia que al menos te reconoce la cara» — "Te reconoce la cara" uses the article where English uses a possessive: Spanish marks the owner with the pronoun "te" instead.
4.1 «El mejor jefe que he tenido» — "El mejor jefe que he tenido" combines a superlative with a relative clause, and "que" cannot be dropped.
4.2 «no decía casi nada» — "No decía casi nada" needs both "no" and "nada", the double negation Spanish requires and English forbids.
4.3 «en las reuniones» — "En las reuniones" keeps the article, where English says "in meetings" with none.
4.4 «Dejaba» — The imperfect "dejaba" describes a repeated habit, which is how Spanish renders the English "would let".
4.5 «que los ruidosos» — "Los ruidosos" is an adjective used as a noun with the article, "the loud ones".

4.7 «Luego hacía» — "Luego hacía" keeps the imperfect for the same repeated habit. "Hacer una pregunta" is the collocation, not "preguntar una pregunta".
4.8 «una pregunta» — "Una pregunta" with the indefinite article, and "una" here carries the sense of "a single one".
4.9 «que recolocaba la sala sin ruido» — "Que recolocaba la sala" opens a relative clause in the imperfect. "Sin ruido" is a noun phrase where English uses the adverb "quietly".
4.10 «Tardé años» — "Tardar años" means "to take years", with the person as the subject. English says "it took me years", with an impersonal "it".
4.11 «en darme cuenta» — "Tardar en" plus the infinitive needs "en". "Darme cuenta" attaches the pronoun to the infinitive.
4.12 «de que aquello era una técnica, no un carácter» — "De que" carries the "de" demanded by "darse cuenta". "Aquello" is the far demonstrative, which distances the memory.
4.13 «Lo he intentado» — "Lo he intentado" uses the neuter "lo" for the whole approach, with the present perfect.
4.14 «unas cuatro veces» — "Unas cuatro veces" uses "unas" for approximation — the feminine plural of "uno" agreeing with "veces".
4.15 «y en tres ocasiones funcionó» — "En tres ocasiones" takes "en", where English uses "on".
4.16 «La cuarta» — "La cuarta" drops the noun "vez", which is understood, and keeps the feminine agreement.
4.17 «hice la pregunta demasiado pronto» — "Demasiado pronto" places "demasiado" before the adverb, and it stays invariable there.
4.18 «y sonó simplemente maleducado» — "Sonó maleducado" uses an adjective after a linking verb, agreeing with the implied masculine subject. "Simplemente" softens it.
4.19 «El momento oportuno» — "El momento oportuno" places the adjective after the noun. Spanish has no single word for "timing".
4.20 «por lo visto» — "Por lo visto" is a fixed parenthetical phrase, "apparently".
4.21 «es lo único que nadie puede enseñarte» — "Lo único que nadie puede enseñarte" uses the neuter "lo" with an adjective, and "nadie" after the verb needs no extra "no" because the clause already begins negatively enough with "que".

10.2 «qué me diría a mí mismo» — "Qué me diría a mí mismo" keeps the accent on "qué" and doubles the pronoun: "me" and "a mí mismo" both appear, which is how Spanish gives emphasis.
10.3 «a los veinticinco» — "A los veinticinco" uses "a los" for the age, and the article is part of the construction.
10.4 «La respuesta honesta» — "La respuesta honesta" places the adjective after the noun.
10.5 «es que no me habría hecho caso» — "No me habría hecho caso" uses the compound conditional for something that did not happen. "Hacer caso" is a fixed phrase, and it takes an indirect object.
10.6 «y habría hecho bien» — "Habría hecho bien" keeps the compound conditional and drops the subject, which is understood.
10.7 «Casi todo lo que sé ahora» — "Casi todo lo que sé ahora" uses the neuter "lo que" after "todo".
10.8 «lo sé porque ignoré» — "Lo sé porque ignoré" doubles the object with "lo", because the fronted "todo lo que sé" is repeated by the pronoun. Spanish requires that doubling.
10.9 «justamente ese tipo de consejos» — "Ese tipo de consejos" links the nouns with "de", and "justamente" intensifies the demonstrative.
10.10 «Los errores» — "Los errores" keeps the article for a general statement.
10.11 «no fueron desvíos» — "No fueron desvíos" uses the preterite for a closed judgement.
10.12 «eran el camino» — "Eran el camino" switches to the imperfect, and the switch is the point: the preterite closed the first half, the imperfect opens the mistakes out into something that lasted.
10.13 «Dicho esto» — "Dicho esto" is an absolute participle construction, "that said".
10.14 «probablemente sí mencionaría» — "Probablemente sí mencionaría" uses "sí" as an emphatic particle, not as "yes". It has no English equivalent and simply strengthens the verb.
10.15 «lo del sueño» — "Lo del sueño" uses the neuter article with "de", meaning "the thing about sleep". It is a compact construction Spanish uses constantly.
10.16 «Se habría reído» — "Se habría reído" is the compound conditional of a reflexive verb, with the pronoun before the auxiliary.
10.17 «habría tomado nota» — "Habría tomado nota" repeats the auxiliary, which Spanish does more readily than English. "Tomar nota" takes no article.
10.18 «y lo habría olvidado para el jueves» — "Lo habría olvidado para el jueves" uses "para" for a deadline, and "lo" for the whole matter.
10.19 «Que es, más o menos» — "Que es, más o menos" begins the sentence with a relative referring to everything before it, with the parenthetical set off by commas.
10.20 «lo que hice yo» — "Lo que hice yo" places the pronoun after the verb for emphasis, which Spanish can do freely.
10.21 «con todo lo que me dijeron» — "Con todo lo que me dijeron" ends with a subjectless plural verb, where English used the passive "I was told".
