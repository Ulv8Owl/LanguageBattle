1.1 «La evaluación de desempeño» — "La evaluación de desempeño" is the standard term for a performance review, and the two nouns are joined with "de". The article is compulsory for a subject noun.
1.2 «salió más o menos tan bien» — "Salir bien" means "to turn out well". "Tan bien como" is the comparison of equality, and "más o menos" softens it — Spanish needs all three words for the effect English gets from "about as well as".
1.3 «como salen siempre estas cosas» — "Como salen siempre estas cosas" puts the verb before the subject, which Spanish does freely in subordinate clauses. "Siempre" sits between them without any awkwardness.
1.4 «Me dijeron» — "Me dijeron" is a subjectless plural verb, the natural equivalent of the English passive "I was told". Spanish cannot make the person the subject of a passive here.
1.5 «que tengo «excelentes dotes de comunicación»» — "Tener" plus a noun for a quality. The clause needs "que", which unlike English "that" can never be dropped. The quotation goes in angle quotes.
1.6 «lo que por lo visto significa que contesto correos» — "Lo que" refers back to the whole quotation, and that is what the neuter "lo" signals. "Por lo visto" means "apparently", and "correos" is short for "correos electrónicos".
1.7 «También hubo» — "Hubo" is the preterite of the impersonal "haber": it never agrees with what follows, so it is never "hubieron".
1.8 «una sugerencia delicada» — "Una sugerencia delicada" places the adjective after the noun. "Delicada" here means "gently put", not "fragile".
1.9 «de que podría ser más estratégico» — "De que" is compulsory after "sugerencia": the noun demands the preposition, and dropping it is a common mistake even among native speakers. "Podría" is the conditional, softening the whole thing.
1.10 «Nadie me ha explicado nunca» — "Nadie" before the verb takes no extra "no", but "nunca" after it is perfectly normal — Spanish allows the accumulation that English forbids. "Me ha explicado" uses the present perfect for experience up to now.
1.11 «qué significa esa palabra» — The indirect question keeps the written accent on "qué" even though there is no question mark.
1.12 «en la práctica» — "En la práctica" keeps the article, unlike English "in practice", which has none.
1.13 «Asentí» — The preterite for a completed action. The written accent on "asentí" marks the stressed final syllable.
1.14 «como se asiente» — "Como se asiente" uses the impersonal "se" where English uses "you". "Asentir" is stem-changing: "asiento", "asientes", "asiente".
1.15 «cuando la alternativa es una conversación más larga» — "Cuando" with the indicative, because this is a general truth rather than a future event. "Más larga" builds the comparative with "más".
1.16 «En conjunto» — "En conjunto" means "on balance, taken together".
1.17 «yo diría que fueron» — "Yo diría" adds the pronoun for emphasis, which Spanish does only when it wants to. The conditional softens the verdict.
1.18 «cuarenta minutos productivos» — "Fueron cuarenta minutos" keeps the verb in the plural, agreeing with the minutes. The adjective follows the noun: "minutos productivos".
2.1 «Me topé» — "Toparse con" is reflexive and takes "con". The preterite places it at a definite moment.
2.2 «con la típica meseta» — "La típica meseta" puts the adjective before the noun, which with "típico" adds a note of resignation. After it, "la meseta típica", it would sound merely descriptive.
2.3 «más o menos en el B1» — "En el B1" keeps the article before the level name, which English does not. "Más o menos" softens the reference.
2.4 «Todo lo que decía» — "Todo lo que" is a three-part frame using the neuter "lo". The verb "decía" is imperfect, for something that went on.
2.5 «era técnicamente correcto» — "Ser" for a lasting property, in the imperfect. "Técnicamente" means "strictly speaking" here.
2.6 «y completamente inerte» — The second adjective shares the same verb. "Inerte" is stronger and more literary than "sin vida".
2.7 «Lo que por fin cambió algo» — "Lo que" opens a subject clause again. "Por fin" means "finally, at last".
2.8 «no fue un manual» — "No fue un manual" negates the copula. This is the first half of the "no ... sino ..." frame.
2.9 «sino un podcast de fútbol» — "Sino" is the word for "but" after a negative and cannot be replaced by "pero". "De fútbol" links the nouns with "de".
2.10 «La mitad» — "La mitad" keeps its article and takes a singular verb even when what follows is plural.
2.11 «se me escapaba por completo» — "Escapársele algo a alguien" is a three-part idiom: the thing escapes, "se" marks it as unintentional, and "me" says to whom. English says "went over my head", which is built quite differently.
2.12 «y resulta que ahí estaba la clave» — "Resulta que" is an impersonal frame, "it turns out that". "Ahí estaba la clave" puts the verb before the subject, which Spanish does for emphasis.
2.13 «El ritmo del idioma» — "El ritmo del idioma" contracts "de" and "el" into "del". Both nouns keep their articles.
2.14 «se coge mucho antes» — "Se coge" is the impersonal "se", "one picks up". "Mucho antes" uses "mucho" to intensify.
2.15 «que el vocabulario» — After a comparison comes "que". "El vocabulario" keeps the article, where English drops it.
2.16 «Ahora sueno» — "Sonar" is stem-changing: "sueno", "suenas", "suena". It is a linking verb here.
2.17 «casi como una persona» — "Como una persona" uses "como" for the comparison, with the article. English says "like a person" with a different word class.
2.18 «al menos en los días buenos» — "Al menos" means "at least". "En los días buenos" keeps the article and puts the adjective after the noun.
3.1 «Mudarse de ciudad» — "Mudarse de ciudad" is reflexive and takes "de". As a subject Spanish uses the infinitive, where English uses the "-ing" form.
3.2 «pasados los treinta» — "Pasados los treinta" is an absolute participle construction: literally "the thirties having passed". The participle agrees with what follows.
3.3 «es una forma rara de empezar de cero» — "Una forma rara de empezar de cero" chains two prepositions with "de", each doing a different job. "Empezar de cero" is the fixed phrase for starting over.
3.4 «Uno se lleva» — "Uno" is the impersonal subject where English uses "you". "Llevarse" is reflexive here, "to take along with oneself".
3.5 «los mismos problemas» — "Los mismos" always keeps the article, exactly as English keeps "the same".
3.6 «solo cambian las paredes» — "Solo" as an adverb means "only" and needs no accent. The verb comes before the subject, which Spanish allows freely.
3.7 «Los dos primeros meses» — "Los dos primeros meses" puts the cardinal before the ordinal — the opposite of English "the first two months". This order is fixed.
3.8 «estaba convencido» — "Estar convencido" uses "estar", because it is a state. The imperfect describes how things were over that period.
3.9 «de que me había equivocado» — "De que" is compulsory after "convencido". "Me había equivocado" is the pluperfect of a reflexive verb, with the pronoun before the auxiliary.
3.10 «Luego un camarero» — "Luego" means "then, afterwards". "Un camarero" has the indefinite article because he appears for the first time.
3.11 «empezó a acordarse de mi pedido» — "Empezar a" keeps its compulsory "a", and "acordarse de" its compulsory "de" — two prepositions in a row, each belonging to a different verb.
3.12 «y algo se colocó en silencio» — "Colocarse" is reflexive: something settled by itself. "En silencio" means "quietly", and Spanish uses a noun phrase where English uses an adverb.
3.13 «Resulta que» — "Resulta que" again, the impersonal frame from the second phrase.
3.14 «sentirse de un sitio» — "Sentirse de un sitio" is an infinitive acting as subject. "Sentirse de" with a place is idiomatic and hard to translate literally.
3.15 «es sobre todo repetición» — "Sobre todo" means "mostly". "Repetición" needs no article here because it is a bare predicate.
3.16 «Todavía echo de menos» — "Echar de menos" is a three-word idiom that cannot be broken up. "Todavía" means "still".
3.17 «el piso antiguo» — "El piso antiguo" puts the adjective after the noun, where it means "old". Before it, "el antiguo piso", it would mean "my former flat" — and here both readings almost work, which is why the position matters.
3.18 «sobre todo por la luz» — "Por la luz" uses "por" for the cause, not "para". Confusing the two is the classic mistake.
4.1 «Le pedimos» — "Pedir" takes an indirect object, so the pronoun is "le". The person is not named again because the previous context makes it clear.
4.2 «un formulario sencillo» — "Un formulario sencillo" places the adjective after the noun. "Sencillo" means "simple, uncomplicated", while "simple" can suggest "simple-minded".
4.3 «a ser posible para el jueves» — "A ser posible" is a fixed phrase, "if possible". "Para el jueves" uses "para" for a deadline; "por el jueves" would mean "around Thursday".
4.4 «Lo que llegó» — "Lo que llegó" is a subject clause with the neuter "lo", and the verb agrees in the singular.
4.5 «tres semanas después» — "Tres semanas después" needs no preposition, and "después" follows the period.
4.6 «era un pequeño y precioso framework» — The imperfect "era" for a description. "Pequeño y precioso" places both adjectives before the noun, which in Spanish makes them evaluative rather than classifying.
4.7 «Contemplaba» — "Contemplar" here means "to cover, to provide for" — a formal use quite different from "to look at".
4.8 «todos los casos» — "Todos los casos" keeps the article after "todos", which is compulsory.
4.9 «que nunca habíamos mencionado» — "Que nunca habíamos mencionado" uses the pluperfect and puts "nunca" before the verb, where it needs no extra "no".
4.10 «Para ser del todo justos» — "Para ser del todo justos" is a fixed parenthetical phrase, and "del todo" means "entirely".
4.11 «el código» — "El código" keeps the article, as any subject noun does.
4.12 «es realmente elegante» — "Ser" for a lasting quality. "Realmente" means "genuinely".
4.13 «Y es también» — "Y es también" places "también" after the verb.
4.14 «lo único» — "Lo único" uses the neuter article with an adjective, "the only thing". This is one of the constructions English has no direct equivalent for.
4.15 «que nadie más sabe mantener» — "Nadie más" puts "más" after the negative pronoun. "Saber" plus an infinitive means "to know how to", where English would use "can".
4.16 «Ahí hay una lección» — "Ahí hay" combines the adverb with the impersonal "hay", which never changes form.
4.17 «que yo mismo» — "Yo mismo" adds the pronoun for emphasis and agrees in gender: a woman would say "yo misma".
4.18 «sigo sin aprender» — "Seguir sin" plus the infinitive means "to still not have done something". It is a compact construction Spanish uses constantly and English lacks.
5.1 «Mi padre» — "Mi padre" uses a possessive instead of an article.
5.2 «ha desarrollado» — The present perfect "ha desarrollado" for something that has taken shape and still holds. In Spain this tense is the natural choice here.
5.3 «una relación complicada con su tableta» — "Una relación complicada con" places the adjective after the noun and takes "con". "Su tableta" uses the possessive, which is ambiguous between "his" and "her" in Spanish.
5.4 «Cada notificación» — "Cada" is invariable and takes a singular noun.
5.5 «la vive como un desafío personal» — "La vive como" uses the feminine object pronoun for "notificación", placed before the verb. "Vivir algo como" means "to experience it as".
5.6 «a su autoridad» — "A su autoridad" takes "a" because "desafío" demands it. That government has to be learned with the noun.
5.7 «Cuando voy a verle» — "Ir a ver a alguien" chains two "a": one for the purpose and one for the person. The pronoun attaches to the infinitive.
5.8 «pasamos veinte minutos» — "Pasar" plus a period means "to spend time". The subject is left unsaid.
5.9 «resolviendo crisis que no consigo reproducir» — The gerund "resolviendo" expresses what the time is spent on. "Conseguir" plus the infinitive means "to manage to", and the relative "que" cannot be dropped.
5.10 «El mes pasado» — "El mes pasado" keeps the article and takes no preposition.
5.11 «me mandó un audio» — "Mandar" takes an indirect object, "me". "Un audio" is the everyday word for a voice message.
5.12 «que eran cuatro segundos de respiración» — "Que eran" puts the verb in the plural, agreeing with "cuatro segundos" rather than with "audio". Spanish agrees with the predicate here, which surprises English speakers.
5.13 «He dejado de explicar» — "Dejar de" plus the infinitive means "to stop doing". The "de" is compulsory.
5.14 «y he empezado» — The auxiliary is repeated: "he dejado ... y he empezado". Spanish repeats it more readily than English does.
5.15 «simplemente a arreglar cosas» — "Empezar a" keeps its "a", and "simplemente" slips in between the auxiliary and the preposition without breaking anything.
5.16 «Él está más contento» — "Él está más contento" uses "estar" for a state and adds the pronoun for contrast. Without "él" the sentence would still be correct but flatter.
5.17 «yo tardo menos» — "Tardar menos" means "to take less time". The parallel structure with the previous element is deliberate.
5.18 «y ninguno tiene que admitir nada» — "Ninguno" takes a singular verb. "Nada" after it needs no extra "no", because the negation is already before the verb.
6.1 «El taller» — "El taller" keeps the article. The word means both "workshop" as an event and "workshop" as a repair shop.
6.2 «se vendió» — "Se vendió como" is the impersonal "se", literally "it sold itself as". English says "was billed as", using a passive.
6.3 «como una sesión de alineación estratégica» — "Una sesión de alineación estratégica" chains the nouns with "de" and puts the adjective last. The order is exactly the reverse of English.
6.4 «En la práctica» — "En la práctica" keeps the article, as in the first phrase.
6.5 «fueron once personas» — "Fueron once personas" puts the verb in the plural, agreeing with the people. English would use the singular "it was".
6.6 «poniéndose ruidosamente de acuerdo en nada» — "Ponerse de acuerdo en" is a four-part idiom, and every piece is needed. The gerund "poniéndose" carries the reflexive pronoun attached to the end.
6.7 «Alguien puso» — "Alguien" is invariable and always singular.
6.8 «la palabra «sinergia»» — "La palabra" keeps its article because the word is named immediately after. Spanish uses angle quotes.
6.9 «en una diapositiva de verdad» — "De verdad" after the noun means "an actual one, a real one". Before the noun it would not work at all.
6.10 «Me gustaría decir» — "Me gustaría decir" is the polite conditional of "gustar", which works backwards: the infinitive is the subject.
6.11 «que protesté» — "Que protesté" needs its "que". The preterite marks a single act.
6.12 «pero sobre todo estuve mirando el reloj» — "Estuve mirando" is the preterite of "estar" plus a gerund, describing a completed stretch of time. The imperfect "estaba mirando" would leave it open-ended.
6.13 «Salimos» — "Salir" without a complement means "to leave". The preterite for a finished action.
6.14 «con cuatro puntos de acción» — "Puntos de acción" links the nouns with "de".
6.15 «dos de ellos con responsable» — "Dos de ellos" uses "de ellos" where a relative clause would be heavier. "Con responsable" needs no article: the noun is indefinite and singular by convention here.
6.16 «Para el viernes» — "Para el viernes" uses "para" for a deadline, as in the fourth phrase.
6.17 «todo aquello» — "Todo aquello" uses the far demonstrative, which puts distance between the speaker and the events.
6.18 «se había evaporado sin ruido» — The pluperfect "se había evaporado", with the reflexive marking that it vanished by itself. "Sin ruido" is a noun phrase where English uses the adverb "quietly".
7.1 «Pasé» — "Pasar por" means "to go through". The preterite places it as a finished period.
7.2 «por una época» — "Una época" has the indefinite article because it is mentioned for the first time.
7.3 «en la que me tomaba el pan muy en serio» — "En la que" places the preposition before the relative, which is compulsory in careful Spanish. "Tomarse algo en serio" is reflexive and idiomatic.
7.4 «Había una masa madre» — "Había" is the imperfect of the impersonal "haber" and never agrees with what follows: never "habían".
7.5 «en la nevera» — "En la nevera" keeps the article. In Latin America the word would be "el refrigerador".
7.6 «con nombre y horario de alimentación» — "Con nombre y horario" drops the articles because the nouns are indefinite and paired. Adding them would change the rhythm and the sense.
7.7 «Durante unas seis semanas» — "Durante unas seis semanas" uses "durante" for the duration and "unas" for the approximation — the feminine plural of "uno" doing the work of English "about".
7.8 «mi cocina» — "Mi cocina" uses a possessive instead of an article.
7.9 «olía verdaderamente de maravilla» — "Oler" is stem-changing and adds an "h": "huelo", "hueles", "huele". "De maravilla" is a fixed phrase, "wonderfully".
7.10 «Luego llegó el trabajo» — "Llegar el trabajo" puts the verb before the subject, which Spanish uses to introduce something new.
7.11 «y la masa madre» — "La masa madre" keeps the article, and both words together are the fixed term for a sourdough starter.
7.12 «tuvo un final discreto» — "Tener un final" is the collocation, not "hacer". "Discreto" placed after the noun keeps its plain meaning.
7.13 «Le digo a la gente» — "Decir a la gente" needs the redundant pronoun "le" as well: Spanish doubles the indirect object even when it is named. "La gente" takes a singular verb.
7.14 «que volveré a ello» — "Volver a algo" takes "a", and "ello" is the neuter pronoun for a whole idea — rather formal, and mostly written.
7.15 «cuando las cosas se calmen» — "Cuando" plus the subjunctive "se calmen" because it points to the future. This is the rule English marks with nothing at all, and it is the single most useful subjunctive to get right.
7.16 «Los dos sabemos» — "Los dos" means "both of us" and keeps the article.
7.17 «que las cosas» — "Que las cosas" opens the subordinate clause, and the article is compulsory in a general statement.
7.18 «no se calman» — "No se calman" uses the indicative, because now it is a statement of fact rather than a projection. The contrast with the subjunctive two elements earlier is the whole point of the joke.
8.1 «Nos conocemos» — "Conocerse" is reciprocal: "we know each other". The pronoun comes before the verb.
8.2 «desde hace tanto» — "Desde hace tanto" uses "desde hace" for a stretch of time up to now, with the verb in the present. English needs a perfect tense here.
8.3 «que ya nos saltamos las novedades» — "Tanto ... que" is a two-part frame of consequence. "Saltarse" is reflexive and means "to skip".
8.4 «Llama» — "Llamar" with no subject pronoun: the ending is enough, and Spanish only adds the pronoun for contrast.
8.5 «cada varios meses» — "Cada varios meses" is colloquial and means "every few months". "Cada" stays invariable.
8.6 «normalmente mientras conduce» — "Mientras conduce" uses a full clause with the verb, where English can drop the subject and say "while driving".
8.7 «Nada importante» — "Nada importante" places the adjective after the pronoun, and "importante" has one form for both genders.
8.8 «se dice» — "Se dice" is the impersonal "se", equivalent to the English passive "is said".
8.9 «en los primeros diez minutos» — "Los primeros diez minutos" puts the ordinal before the cardinal here, the reverse of the third phrase — because "primeros" is doing a different job in each.
8.10 «Luego, cerca del final» — "Cerca del final" contracts "de" and "el".
8.11 «se le escapa» — "Escapársele a alguien" again, as in the second phrase: "se le escapa" has both pronouns, and neither can be dropped.
8.12 «una cosa de verdad» — "Una cosa de verdad" places "de verdad" after the noun, meaning "a real one".
8.13 «Ninguno de los dos» — "Ninguno de los dos" takes a singular verb, even though it refers to two people.
8.14 «lo comenta» — "Lo comenta" uses the neuter "lo" for the whole thing, placed before the verb. Spanish needs no preposition after "comentar".
8.15 «y en eso consiste todo el acuerdo» — "Consistir en" always takes "en". "Todo el acuerdo" keeps the article after "todo".
8.16 «Me costaría» — "Costarle a alguien" works backwards, like "gustar": the difficulty is the subject and the person is the indirect object. The conditional makes it hypothetical.
8.17 «explicar esa amistad» — The infinitive is the subject of "costaría". "Esa amistad" uses the demonstrative instead of the article.
8.18 «a alguien menor de treinta» — "A alguien" keeps the personal "a". "Menor de treinta" uses "de" after the comparative, not "que" — a genuine trap, since "que" is right in other comparisons.
