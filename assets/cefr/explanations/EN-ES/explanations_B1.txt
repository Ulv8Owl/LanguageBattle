1.1 «Llevo trabajando» — "Llevar" plus a gerund is how Spanish says "have been doing": "llevo trabajando" means "I have been working". There is no perfect tense here at all — the verb is in the present, and "llevar" carries the duration.
1.2 «en el mismo equipo» — "El mismo" always takes the article, and it agrees: "el mismo equipo", "la misma casa". Placed after the noun, "el equipo mismo", it would mean "the team itself".
1.3 «tres años» — After "llevar" the length of time needs no preposition: "llevo trabajando tres años". English "for" has nothing to translate into here.
1.4 «Últimamente» — "Últimamente" means "recently" and pairs naturally with the present perfect. Adverbs in "-mente" are built on the feminine form of the adjective.
1.5 «he empezado» — Spanish uses the present perfect for something recent whose effect is still felt: "he empezado". In Spain this tense is far more common than in Latin America, where the simple past would often be used instead.
1.6 «a sentirme un poco estancado» — After "empezar" the preposition "a" is compulsory before the infinitive: "empezar a sentirme". And the reflexive pronoun attaches to the end of the infinitive, "sentirme", where a conjugated verb would take it in front.
1.7 «Mi jefe» — "Mi jefe" with a possessive instead of an article. The word covers "boss" and "manager" alike.
1.8 «me propuso un curso corto» — "Proponer" takes an indirect object: "me propuso". "Un curso corto" has the adjective after the noun, and "un" because the course is new to the conversation.
1.9 «y me apunté la semana pasada» — "Apuntarse" is reflexive, hence "me". "La semana pasada" keeps its article and takes no preposition — the adjective after the noun does the work of English "last".
1.10 «Si sale bien» — In an "si" clause about a real possibility Spanish uses the present indicative, never the future: "si sale bien". English follows the same rule, so this one transfers cleanly.
1.11 «quizá me presente» — "Quizá" is usually followed by the subjunctive when the speaker is genuinely unsure: "quizá me presente". With the indicative it would sound closer to a prediction.
1.12 «a otro puesto» — "Presentarse a" takes "a" for the post you apply for. "Otro" never takes an article in Spanish: "otro puesto", never "un otro puesto".
2.1 «Nos mudamos» — "Mudarse" is reflexive and means moving house specifically; "mover" would be moving an object. The pronoun "nos" goes before the conjugated verb.
2.2 «a un piso nuevo» — Direction with "a": "nos mudamos a un piso". "Nuevo" after the noun means "brand new"; before it, "un nuevo piso", it would mean "another one".
2.3 «en abril» — Months take "en": "en abril", written in lower case.
2.4 «El edificio» — "El" because it is the building of the flat already mentioned. Spanish keeps the article where English uses "the" for exactly the same reason.
2.5 «es antiguo» — "Ser" for a lasting property: "es antiguo". "Antiguo" after the noun means "old"; before it, it would mean "former".
2.6 «así que las cosas se rompen a menudo» — "Así que" introduces the consequence. "Romperse" is reflexive here: things break by themselves, and Spanish marks that with "se" where English changes nothing.
2.7 «El mes pasado» — "El mes pasado" keeps the article and takes no preposition.
2.8 «la calefacción» — "La calefacción" with the article, as any subject noun takes one in Spanish. The word is feminine, like most nouns ending in "-ción".
2.9 «dejó de funcionar una semana» — "Dejar de" plus the infinitive means "to stop doing": "dejó de funcionar". The preposition "de" is compulsory. "Una semana" gives the duration with no preposition at all.
2.10 «El casero» — "El casero" is the landlord. The article is enough to identify him; a possessive would sound odd here.
2.11 «lo arregló al final» — "Lo" is the masculine object pronoun and goes before the conjugated verb: "lo arregló". "Al final" means "in the end" and contracts "a" plus "el".
2.12 «pero tardó demasiado» — "Tardar" means "to take time": "tardó demasiado". "Demasiado" here is an adverb and does not agree with anything.
3.1 «Llevo aprendiendo español» — "Llevar" plus a gerund again, this time with no preposition before the language: "llevo aprendiendo español". Language names are lower case in Spanish.
3.2 «casi un año» — "Casi" goes before what it qualifies. "Un año" keeps its article: a single period always takes one.
3.3 «sobre todo en el móvil» — "Sobre todo" means "mostly, above all". "En el móvil" uses "en" and the article; in Latin America the word would be "el celular".
3.4 «Puedo leer» — "Poder" is stem-changing: "puedo", "puedes", "puede". After it the infinitive follows directly, with no preposition.
3.5 «menús y artículos cortos» — "Menús" takes a written accent in the singular and keeps it in the plural. Neither noun takes an article, because they are indefinite quantities, and the adjective follows the noun.
3.6 «sin mucha dificultad» — "Sin" plus a noun with no article: "sin mucha dificultad". "Dificultad" is feminine, so "mucha" agrees with it.
3.7 «Hablar» — The infinitive is used as a subject in Spanish where English uses the "-ing" form: "hablar" means "speaking" here.
3.8 «sigue siendo lo difícil» — "Seguir" plus a gerund means "to still be": "sigue siendo". And "lo difícil" uses the neuter article "lo" with an adjective to mean "the hard part" — a construction English has no direct equivalent for.
3.9 «sobre todo por teléfono» — "Por teléfono" uses "por" and takes no article, unlike English "on the phone".
3.10 «El verano que viene» — "El verano que viene" keeps the article and needs no preposition. Literally it is "the summer that comes".
3.11 «me gustaría pasar» — "Gustar" works backwards, and in the conditional it becomes a polite "I would like": "me gustaría". The verb stays singular because its subject is the infinitive that follows.
3.12 «dos semanas en Valencia» — "Pasar" with a length of time takes no preposition: "pasar dos semanas". The city takes "en": "en Valencia".
4.1 «Nuestro vuelo» — "Nuestro" agrees with the masculine "vuelo". Unlike "mi", this possessive does change for gender and number.
4.2 «se retrasó» — "Retrasarse" with "se" presents the delay as something that happened to the flight, with nobody to blame. English uses the passive "was delayed" for the same effect.
4.3 «cuatro horas» — The length of the delay takes no preposition: "se retrasó cuatro horas". English needs "by"; Spanish needs nothing.
4.4 «Nadie» — "Nadie" is a negative pronoun. When it comes before the verb, as here, no second negative word is needed; after the verb it would require "no" in front of it.
4.5 «en el mostrador» — "En el mostrador" uses "en" for the counter. The article is compulsory.
4.6 «supo decirnos por qué» — "Supo" is the preterite of "saber", and in the preterite it means "managed to, was able to" rather than simply "knew". "Decirnos" attaches the pronoun to the infinitive.
4.7 «Al final» — "Al final" means "in the end", with "a" and "el" contracted.
4.8 «nos dieron» — "Dar" is irregular in the preterite: "di", "dio", "dieron". The indirect object pronoun "nos" comes first and cannot be dropped, even though the person is obvious.
4.9 «un vale para un bocadillo» — "Un vale para un bocadillo": "para" expresses what it is for. Both articles are indefinite because both things are new to the conversation.
4.10 «He decidido» — The present perfect for a decision that still stands: "he decidido". In Spain this is the natural tense; "decidí" would place it further in the past.
4.11 «volar con otra compañía» — After "decidir" the infinitive follows directly with no preposition. "Con otra compañía" uses "otra" with no article, as Spanish always does.
4.12 «la próxima vez» — "La próxima vez" keeps its article, where English says "next time" with nothing at all.
5.1 «Antes tocaba» — "Antes" plus the imperfect is how Spanish says "used to": "antes tocaba". There is no separate construction, so the tense alone carries the meaning of a past habit that has ended.
5.2 «la guitarra» — Musical instruments take the article after "tocar": "tocar la guitarra". And "tocar" is the verb for playing an instrument, while "jugar" is for games.
5.3 «cuando estaba en el colegio» — "Estar en el colegio" uses "estar" for a temporary situation, with the article. "Colegio" is school for children; "escuela" and "instituto" cover other stages.
5.4 «Lo dejé» — "Lo" here is the neuter pronoun standing for the whole activity, not for the guitar. That is why it is "lo" and not "la".
5.5 «cuando empecé a trabajar» — "Empezar a" plus the infinitive, with the compulsory "a". The preterite "empecé" is spelled with "c" before "e".
5.6 «y luego me arrepentí» — "Arrepentirse" is reflexive and takes "de" when it has an object. "Luego" means "afterwards" here rather than "later on".
5.7 «Un amigo» — "Un amigo" with the indefinite article: one friend among several. "Mi amigo" would point at a particular one already known.
5.8 «me prestó la suya vieja» — "Prestar" is to lend and "pedir prestado" is to borrow — Spanish needs a phrase where English has the single verb "borrow". "La suya" is a possessive pronoun standing alone, agreeing with the feminine "guitarra".
5.9 «en enero» — Months with "en": "en enero".
5.10 «Ahora» — "Ahora" opens the contrast with the past and takes no preposition.
5.11 «practico» — "Practicar" in the present for a current habit. The written accent is only in some forms, not in "practico".
5.12 «veinte minutos cada tarde» — The duration takes no preposition: "veinte minutos". "Cada tarde" also needs none, and "cada" is invariable — it never becomes "cadas".
6.1 «Mi hermana» — "Mi hermana" with a possessive instead of an article. "Mi" is the same for both genders.
6.2 «acaba de terminar» — "Acabar de" plus the infinitive means "to have just done": "acaba de terminar". Spanish has no adverb like English "just" in this position — the whole idea sits in the verb phrase.
6.3 «el primer año de universidad» — "El primer año" uses the shortened form of "primero" before a masculine noun. "De universidad" joins the two nouns with "de", and here the article is dropped.
6.4 «Estudia» — "Estudia" in the present covers both "she studies" and "she is studying". Spanish does have a continuous form, but it is reserved for what is happening right now.
6.5 «algo relacionado con datos» — "Relacionado con" always takes "con". "Datos" is plural in Spanish, unlike English "data", which behaves as uncountable.
6.6 «que yo no entiendo muy bien» — "Que" introduces the relative clause and, unlike English "which", it can never be dropped. The pronoun "yo" is added here only for emphasis.
6.7 «Cada vez que le pregunto» — "Cada vez que" means "whenever". "Le" is the indirect object pronoun: you ask something to somebody in Spanish, so the person is indirect.
6.8 «me lo explica» — Two pronouns together go indirect first, then direct: "me lo explica". The order is fixed and cannot be swapped.
6.9 «con mucha paciencia» — "Con mucha paciencia" uses a noun where English uses the adverb "patiently". This "con" plus noun pattern is extremely common in Spanish.
6.10 «Sigo» — "Seguir" here means "to still be doing", and it is stem-changing: "sigo", "sigues", "sigue".
6.11 «sin enterarme de nada» — "Sin enterarme de nada" uses an infinitive after "sin", with the pronoun attached to it. "Nada" after a negative-flavoured word needs no extra "no".
6.12 «pero estoy orgulloso de ella igualmente» — "Estar orgulloso de" uses "estar", because it is a state, and "de" for the person. The adjective agrees with the speaker: a woman would say "orgullosa".
7.1 «Teníamos que lanzar» — "Tener que" plus the infinitive expresses obligation, and in the imperfect it hints that the plan did not work out — the same shade as English "were supposed to".
7.2 «la web nueva» — "La web" is feminine and short for "la página web". The adjective follows the noun: "la web nueva".
7.3 «en junio» — Months with "en": "en junio".
7.4 «Entonces el diseñador» — "Entonces" means "then, at that point". "El diseñador" takes the article, as any noun does when it is the subject.
7.5 «se fue de la empresa» — "Irse de" means to leave a place or an organisation, and both the "se" and the "de" are needed: "se fue de la empresa".
7.6 «y todo se paró un mes» — "Todo" as a subject is singular, so the verb agrees in the singular. "Pararse" is reflexive here, and "un mes" gives the duration with no preposition.
7.7 «Por fin» — "Por fin" means "finally", with relief. "Finalmente" would be more neutral and more formal.
7.8 «la publicamos» — "La" is the feminine object pronoun standing for "la web", and it goes before the conjugated verb: "la publicamos".
7.9 «el jueves pasado» — Days of the week take the article and no preposition: "el jueves pasado".
7.10 «De momento» — "De momento" means "for now, so far". It pairs naturally with the present perfect that follows.
7.11 «nadie se ha quejado» — "Nadie" before the verb needs no second negative. "Quejarse" is reflexive and takes "de" when the complaint has an object.
7.12 «lo cual ya parece un triunfo» — "Lo cual" refers back to the whole previous clause, not to a single noun — that is exactly what the neuter "lo" signals. "Ya" here means "already".
8.1 «Intento» — "Intentar" is followed directly by the infinitive, with no preposition. In the present it describes an ongoing effort.
8.2 «llevar un presupuesto» — "Llevar un presupuesto" is the fixed collocation, literally "to carry a budget". The article is compulsory because "presupuesto" is countable.
8.3 «pero nunca me sale del todo» — "Nunca" before the verb needs no extra "no". "Salir" here means "to work out", and "me sale" puts the speaker in the indirect object — the budget is what does or does not work.
8.4 «El problema» — "El problema" is masculine despite ending in "-a": it comes from Greek, like "el tema" and "el sistema".
8.5 «no son los gastos grandes» — The verb agrees with the plural that follows, "los gastos grandes", not with the singular "el problema". Spanish and English differ here, and English would keep the singular.
8.6 «sino los pequeños» — "Sino" is the word for "but" after a negative, and it is not the same as "pero". "Los pequeños" drops the noun but keeps the article and the agreement.
8.7 «El café» — "El café" keeps its article because it is a general subject. English drops it, and that missing article is the usual slip.
8.8 «y los taxis» — "Los taxis" also keeps its article. Spanish needs one before any noun used as a subject in a general statement.
8.9 «suman más rápido de lo que crees» — "Sumar" means "to add up". The comparison uses "más ... de lo que" before a clause, not "que": "más rápido de lo que crees".
8.10 «Este mes» — "Este mes" keeps the demonstrative and takes no preposition or article.
8.11 «he apuntado» — The present perfect for something done within a period that has not ended: "he apuntado". The unfinished month is exactly why this tense is right.
8.12 «cada pago» — "Cada pago" is invariable in "cada" and singular in the noun. Spanish never says "cada pagos".
9.1 «No duermo bien» — Negation is a single "no" before the verb, and "dormir" is stem-changing: "duermo", "duermes", "duerme".
9.2 «si miro el portátil» — In an "si" clause about a repeated condition Spanish uses the present indicative. "Mirar" takes no preposition, unlike English "look at".
9.3 «tarde por la noche» — "Tarde por la noche" combines the adverb with "por la noche"; parts of the day always take "por" plus the article.
9.4 «He probado» — The present perfect again for a recent attempt whose result matters now: "he probado".
9.5 «a dejarlo» — "Probar a" plus the infinitive means "to try doing something as an experiment". The pronoun attaches to the infinitive: "dejarlo".
9.6 «en la cocina» — "En la cocina" with the article, which Spanish keeps where English also uses "the".
9.7 «Funciona» — "Funcionar" means "to work" of a thing or an arrangement. The subject is left unsaid, as Spanish usually does.
9.8 «pero solo» — "Pero solo" limits what follows. Written without an accent, "solo" is the adverb "only".
9.9 «cuando lo hago de verdad» — "Lo hago de verdad" uses "lo" for the whole action. "De verdad" means "really, actually" and follows the verb.
9.10 «Los hábitos» — "Los hábitos" keeps the article for a general statement. The word carries a written accent on the first syllable.
9.11 «son mucho más fáciles de planear» — "Mucho" intensifies the comparative "más fáciles". And "fácil de" plus the infinitive is the fixed pattern: the preposition "de" is compulsory.
9.12 «que de mantener» — After "que" the pattern repeats exactly: "de planear que de mantener". Dropping the second "de" would break the symmetry the construction requires.
10.1 «Mi coche viejo» — "Mi coche viejo" puts the adjective after the noun. Before it, "mi viejo coche", it would sound affectionate rather than descriptive.
10.2 «por fin» — "Por fin" means "at last", with the same relief as in the seventh phrase.
10.3 «murió en febrero» — "Morir" is used figuratively of a car, exactly as in English. It is stem-changing, but the preterite "murió" changes the vowel to "u". Months take "en".
10.4 «En vez de comprar otro» — "En vez de" is followed by the infinitive: "en vez de comprar". Spanish uses the infinitive where English uses the "-ing" form after "instead of".
10.5 «decidí» — The preterite "decidí" for a decision made at a definite moment. The accent on the last syllable is written.
10.6 «probar el tren» — After "decidir" the infinitive follows directly. "El tren" keeps its article, where English says "the train" for the same reason.
10.7 «El viaje» — "El viaje" is masculine despite ending in "-e". The article is compulsory for a subject noun.
10.8 «dura más» — "Durar" means "to last": "dura más". The comparative needs only "más", with no word for "-er".
10.9 «pero puedo leer por el camino» — "Por el camino" means "on the way", with "por" for movement along something. "Poder" is followed directly by the infinitive.
10.10 «No creo» — "No creo" carries the negation in the main clause, just as English does. What follows, however, changes because of it.
10.11 «que vuelva» — After "no creo que" the verb must go into the subjunctive: "vuelva", not "vuelvo". Doubt and denial trigger this mood, and it is the main thing English speakers have to add here.
10.12 «a conducir» — "Volver a" plus the infinitive means "to do something again": "volver a conducir" is "to go back to driving". The "a" is compulsory and part of the construction.
