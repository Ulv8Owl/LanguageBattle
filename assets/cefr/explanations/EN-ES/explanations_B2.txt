1.1 «Llevamos intentando» — "Llevar" plus a gerund is how Spanish expresses "have been doing": "llevamos intentando". The verb is in the present, and there is no perfect tense involved at all.
1.2 «contratar a un segundo diseñador» — "Contratar" takes the personal "a" before a human object, even an indefinite one: "contratar a un diseñador". "Segundo" here means "one more", not the second in a list.
1.3 «desde febrero» — "Desde" marks the starting point: "desde febrero". For a length of time Spanish would say "desde hace tres meses", so the two are not interchangeable.
1.4 «Cada candidato» — "Cada" is invariable and always takes a singular noun: "cada candidato". There is no plural form of the word.
1.5 «que nos gustaba de verdad» — "Gustar" works backwards: the candidate is the subject and "nos" is the indirect object. And the relative "que" can never be dropped, unlike English "that".
1.6 «ya había aceptado otra cosa» — The pluperfect: "había aceptado" places the acceptance before another past moment. "Otra cosa" takes no article — "otro" never does in Spanish.
1.7 «Recursos Humanos nos repite» — "Recursos Humanos" is treated as a singular unit here, so the verb is singular. "Repetir" is stem-changing: "repito", "repites", "repite".
1.8 «que el mercado está difícil» — "Estar difícil" rather than "ser difícil" presents it as the current state of the market, not a permanent quality. That choice is a real difference in meaning, not a stylistic one.
1.9 «y probablemente sea cierto» — After "probablemente" the subjunctive "sea" expresses genuine uncertainty. With the indicative "es" it would sound closer to a statement of fact.
1.10 «Aun así» — "Aun así" means "even so", and "aun" here carries no accent. With an accent, "aún", it would mean "still".
1.11 «sospecho» — "Sospechar" opens the subordinate clause with "que", which is compulsory in Spanish where English can drop "that".
1.12 «que nuestro proceso es demasiado lento» — "Ser lento" for a lasting property. "Demasiado" is invariable when it modifies an adjective, so it does not agree with anything.
1.13 «Si no somos capaces de decidir en menos de dos semanas» — "Ser capaz de" plus the infinitive means "to be able to". "En menos de dos semanas" uses "en" for the period within which something happens.
1.14 «no deberíamos sorprendernos» — The conditional "deberíamos" softens the obligation. "Sorprendernos" is reflexive and the pronoun attaches to the infinitive.
1.15 «cuando la gente se va a otro sitio» — "Cuando" with the indicative refers to something that regularly happens; with the subjunctive it would point to the future. That distinction is one of the hardest for English speakers.
2.1 «El informe» — "El informe" takes the article, as any subject noun does in Spanish.
2.2 «tenía que estar listo» — "Tener que" in the imperfect expresses an obligation that existed and hints it was not met — the same shade as English "was supposed to". "Estar listo" uses "estar" for a state.
2.3 «el lunes» — Days of the week take the article and no preposition: "el lunes". English uses "on" and no article, so both parts differ.
2.4 «Nadie había mencionado» — "Nadie" before the verb needs no second negative word. After the verb it would demand "no" in front of it.
2.5 «que los datos» — "Los datos" is plural in Spanish, unlike English "data", which behaves as uncountable.
2.6 «venían de tres sistemas distintos» — "Venir de" means "to come from". The imperfect "venían" describes an ongoing state within the past.
2.7 «Unificarlos» — The infinitive is used where English uses a gerund as subject: "unificarlos". The object pronoun attaches to the end of the infinitive.
2.8 «nos llevó casi toda la semana» — "Llevar" here means "to take time", and it needs the indirect object "nos". "Casi toda la semana" keeps the article after "toda".
2.9 «y la mitad de las cifras seguía sin cuadrar» — "La mitad de las cifras" takes a singular verb, agreeing with "la mitad". "Seguir sin" plus the infinitive means "to still not be doing something".
2.10 «Al final» — "Al final" means "in the end", with "a" and "el" contracted.
2.11 «lo enviamos» — "Lo" is the masculine object pronoun standing for the report, and it goes before the conjugated verb.
2.12 «con una nota sobre las discrepancias» — "Sobre" introduces the topic. "Las discrepancias" takes the article because they were already mentioned.
2.13 «Para mi sorpresa» — "Para mi sorpresa" is a fixed phrase with "para", set off by a comma.
2.14 «nadie hizo» — "Nadie" again before the verb, so no second negative is needed. "Hacer una pregunta" is the fixed collocation; "preguntar una pregunta" would be wrong.
2.15 «ni una sola pregunta al respecto» — "Ni una sola" reinforces the negation. "Al respecto" is a fixed phrase meaning "about it", again with "a" and "el" contracted.
3.1 «Nunca se me ha dado» — "Dárselo bien a alguien" is an idiom for being good at something, and it works backwards: the skill is the subject and the person is the indirect object, "me". The literal sense is "it has never been given well to me".
3.2 «especialmente bien» — "Especialmente" softens the negation and goes before the adverb "bien".
3.3 «la conversación de cortesía» — The article is compulsory because this is the grammatical subject of "se me ha dado". "De cortesía" links the two nouns with "de".
3.4 «En los congresos» — "En los congresos" keeps the article for a general statement. And "congreso" is the word for a professional conference; "conferencia" is usually a single lecture.
3.5 «suelo acabar» — "Soler" plus the infinitive means "usually to do", and Spanish has no adverb doing that job here. It is stem-changing: "suelo", "sueles", "suele".
3.6 «cerca de la máquina de café» — "Cerca de" needs both words. "La máquina de café" joins the nouns with "de", where English simply puts them side by side.
3.7 «A veces alguien» — "A veces" means "sometimes" and takes no article. "Alguien" is invariable and always singular.
3.8 «empieza la conversación por su cuenta» — "Por su cuenta" means "on their own initiative". "La conversación" takes the article as a normal direct object.
3.9 «y conozco a gente realmente interesante» — "Conocer a" needs the personal "a" before people, and "gente" counts as people even though it is grammatically singular. "Realmente" goes before the adjective.
3.10 «Más a menudo» — "Más a menudo" is the comparative of the adverbial phrase "a menudo". Spanish builds it with "más" rather than with an ending.
3.11 «simplemente me bebo» — "Beberse" with the reflexive pronoun stresses that the whole amount is consumed. Without "me" the sentence would be correct but flatter.
3.12 «mucho café malo» — "Mucho café malo" places the adjective after the noun and takes no article: an indefinite quantity.
3.13 «Es una estrategia» — "Es una estrategia" uses "ser" for identity. The article "una" is compulsory before a countable noun in this position.
3.14 «con defectos evidentes» — "Con" introduces the characteristic. "Defectos evidentes" has the adjective after the noun and no article.
3.15 «pero es la única que tengo» — "La única que tengo" keeps the article and agrees in the feminine with "estrategia". The relative "que" cannot be dropped.
4.1 «Nuestro equipo» — "Nuestro" agrees with the masculine "equipo". Unlike "mi", this possessive changes for gender and number.
4.2 «pasó al teletrabajo» — "Pasar a" plus a noun means "to switch to", and "a" plus "el" contracts into "al". "Teletrabajo" is one word.
4.3 «casi de un día para otro» — "De un día para otro" is a fixed idiom meaning "overnight" in the figurative sense. Neither noun takes an article inside it.
4.4 «Al principio» — "Al principio" means "at first" and contrasts with what came later.
4.5 «todos decían» — "Todos" is plural in Spanish, so the verb is plural too — the opposite of English "everyone", which is singular.
4.6 «que era temporal» — The imperfect "era" for a state within the past. Spanish shifts the tense in reported speech just as English does.
4.7 «Dos años después» — "Después" follows the period with no preposition: "dos años después".
4.8 «la oficina se usa sobre todo» — "Se usa" is the impersonal passive with "se": the doer is not named. "Sobre todo" means "mostly".
4.9 «para reuniones y galletas gratis» — "Para" introduces the purpose. "Gratis" is invariable and never agrees with anything.
4.10 «El trabajo en sí» — "En sí" means "itself" and follows the noun: "el trabajo en sí".
4.11 «no ha cambiado mucho» — The present perfect with "mucho" in a negative sentence. In Spain this tense is the natural choice for something still relevant.
4.12 «pero los desplazamientos han desaparecido» — "Los desplazamientos" keeps the article and goes in the plural, where English uses the singular "commute". The present perfect again marks a result that still holds.
4.13 «Nadie» — "Nadie" as the subject, before the verb, so no second negative word is needed.
4.14 «a quien yo conozca» — "A quien" is the relative for people after a preposition, and "conozca" must be in the subjunctive: the person is hypothetical, not identified. This is exactly the case English marks with nothing at all.
4.15 «quiere volver» — "Querer" is stem-changing: "quiero", "quieres", "quiere". It is followed directly by the infinitive, with no preposition.
5.1 «Mi casero» — "Mi casero" uses a possessive instead of an article. The word means the landlord of a rented flat.
5.2 «subió el alquiler» — "Subir" is used transitively here, "to raise". "El alquiler" is the rent as an amount of money.
5.3 «un diez por ciento» — The size of the rise takes no preposition in Spanish: "subió el alquiler un diez por ciento". English needs "by"; Spanish needs nothing.
5.4 «Me explicó» — "Explicar" takes an indirect object, so the pronoun is "me". The clause that follows needs "que".
5.5 «que los gastos de mantenimiento» — "Los gastos de mantenimiento" joins the nouns with "de" and keeps the article.
5.6 «habían aumentado» — The pluperfect: the costs had risen before he explained it. "Aumentar" is intransitive here.
5.7 «El ascensor» — "El ascensor" takes the article. In Latin America the word is often "el elevador".
5.8 «lleva estropeado desde noviembre» — "Llevar" plus a participle expresses a state that has lasted: "lleva estropeado desde noviembre". Once again the verb is in the present, not in a perfect tense.
5.9 «lo que debilitaba un poco su argumento» — "Lo que" refers back to the whole previous clause, and that is what the neuter "lo" signals. Compare "lo cual", which is more formal but does the same job.
5.10 «Le pregunté educadamente» — "Le" is the indirect object pronoun, because in Spanish you ask something to somebody. "Educadamente" goes after the verb.
5.11 «si la subida» — "Si" introduces an indirect yes-or-no question here, not a condition. "La subida" is a noun formed from "subir".
5.12 «incluía arreglarlo» — After "incluir" the infinitive follows directly, with the object pronoun attached: "arreglarlo".
5.13 «Dijo» — "Decir" is irregular in the preterite: "dije", "dijo", "dijeron". The subject is left unsaid.
5.14 «que lo miraría» — The conditional "miraría" replaces the future in reported speech. "Lo" stands for the whole matter, not for the lift itself.
5.15 «lo cual suele significar que no» — "Lo cual" again refers to everything said before. "Soler" plus the infinitive gives the sense of "usually".
6.1 «Me apunté» — "Apuntarse" is reflexive: you sign yourself up. The preterite places it at a definite moment.
6.2 «a un maratón» — "A un maratón" uses "a" for what you sign up for. The word is masculine in Spain.
6.3 «en un momento de entusiasmo» — "En un momento de entusiasmo" uses "en" for the state one is in, and "de" to link the nouns.
6.4 «El entrenamiento» — "El entrenamiento" is singular in Spanish and keeps its article, where English "training" is uncountable and article-free.
6.5 «va» — "Ir" is used for how something is going, exactly as English uses "go". It is irregular: "voy", "vas", "va".
6.6 «mejor de lo que esperaba» — "Mejor de lo que esperaba" uses "de lo que" before a clause, not "que". Using "que" here is one of the most frequent mistakes.
6.7 «El problema» — "El problema" is masculine despite the "-a" ending, like "el tema" and "el sistema".
6.8 «es que ahora hablo de correr» — "Es que" introduces the explanation. "Hablar de" takes "de", and the infinitive "correr" follows where English uses the "-ing" form.
6.9 «sin parar» — "Sin parar" is a fixed phrase, literally "without stopping". After "sin" Spanish always uses the infinitive.
6.10 «Mis amigos» — "Mis amigos" uses the plural possessive. "Mis" changes only for number, never for gender.
6.11 «han empezado a cambiar de tema» — "Empezar a" plus the infinitive keeps its compulsory "a". "Cambiar de tema" takes "de" and no article.
6.12 «antes de que yo abra la boca» — "Antes de que" always requires the subjunctive: "abra". There is no exception to this rule, and English marks nothing here.
6.13 «Por lo visto» — "Por lo visto" is a fixed phrase meaning "apparently".
6.14 «hay un límite» — "Hay" is the invariable existential form of "haber": it never becomes "han" no matter what follows.
6.15 «a lo que uno puede escuchar sobre ritmos por kilómetro» — "A lo que" carries the "a" demanded by "un límite a". "Uno" is the impersonal subject, where English would say "anyone".
7.1 «El sistema nuevo» — "El sistema nuevo" places the adjective after the noun. Before it, "el nuevo sistema", it would suggest a replacement rather than a new thing.
7.2 «se implantó» — "Se implantó" is the impersonal passive with "se", where English uses "was introduced".
7.3 «casi sin avisar» — "Sin avisar" uses the infinitive after "sin", as Spanish always does.
7.4 «Nos dijeron» — "Nos dijeron" is a plural verb with no stated subject, the natural equivalent of the English passive "we were told". Spanish cannot make the person the subject of a passive here.
7.5 «que ahorraría» — The conditional "ahorraría" replaces the future in reported speech.
7.6 «como una hora al día a cada uno» — "Al día" means "per day", with "a" and "el" contracted. "A cada uno" repeats the preposition for the person.
7.7 «De momento» — "De momento" means "so far, for now".
7.8 «sobre todo ha generado» — "Sobre todo" goes before the verb here. The present perfect suits a period that is still open.
7.9 «nuevos tipos de reuniones» — "Tipos de reuniones" links the nouns with "de". The adjective goes before the noun in "nuevos tipos", which is normal for this kind of word.
7.10 «Para ser justos» — "Para ser justos" is a fixed parenthetical phrase using the infinitive, set off by commas.
7.11 «la interfaz» — "La interfaz" is feminine and ends in "-z", which becomes "-ces" in the plural.
7.12 «ha mejorado de verdad» — "De verdad" means "really" and follows the verb. The present perfect marks the improvement as still valid.
7.13 «Si eso justifica» — "Si" here introduces a noun clause acting as the subject, not a condition. The indicative "justifica" is right because no doubt is being expressed.
7.14 «seis meses de migración» — "Seis meses de migración" links the period to the process with "de".
7.15 «ya es otra cuestión» — "Ya" adds the sense of "that is another matter altogether". "Otra" takes no article, as "otro" never does.
8.1 «Mi vecino» — "Mi vecino" uses a possessive instead of an article.
8.2 «decidió aprender a tocar la trompeta» — Three verbs in a row: "decidió", then "aprender a", then "tocar". The "a" after "aprender" is compulsory, and instruments take "tocar" plus the article.
8.3 «a los cincuenta y dos» — Age is introduced with "a los": "a los cincuenta y dos". The article is part of the construction.
8.4 «El primer mes» — "El primer mes" uses the shortened "primer" before a masculine noun. The article is compulsory.
8.5 «fue, sinceramente» — "Sinceramente" is a parenthetical adverb set off by commas on both sides.
8.6 «bastante duro de escuchar» — "Duro de escuchar" uses "de" plus the infinitive after an adjective. That preposition cannot be dropped.
8.7 «Estuve a punto de decirle algo» — "Estar a punto de" plus the infinitive means "to be about to". "Decirle" attaches the indirect object pronoun to the infinitive.
8.8 «y entonces me acordé» — "Acordarse de" is reflexive and takes "de". Without the pronoun, "recordar", the preposition disappears.
8.9 «de lo mal que canto yo» — "De lo mal que canto" uses the neuter "lo" with an adverb to mean "how badly". The "de" comes from "acordarse de".
8.10 «Ha mejorado muchísimo» — The present perfect for an improvement that still holds. "Muchísimo" is the absolute superlative of "mucho".
8.11 «desde el verano» — "Desde el verano" marks the starting point and keeps the article before the season.
8.12 «y estoy curiosamente orgulloso de él» — "Estar orgulloso de" uses "estar" for a state and "de" for the person. "Curiosamente" modifies the adjective.
8.13 «Ahora» — "Ahora" opens the contrast with the past.
8.14 «el silencio de los domingos» — "El silencio de los domingos" links the nouns with "de" and keeps the plural article for a habitual day.
8.15 «resulta casi extraño» — "Resultar" is a copular verb here, "to turn out to be", so an adjective follows, not an adverb.
9.1 «No suelo» — "Soler" in the negative: "no suelo" means "I don't usually". The negation goes before the verb, and there is no auxiliary.
9.2 «fiarme de los consejos de productividad» — "Fiarse de" is reflexive and takes "de". "Los consejos de productividad" keeps the article and links the nouns with "de".
9.3 «sobre todo porque se contradicen entre sí» — "Entre sí" is the fixed reciprocal phrase, "with each other". "Se contradicen" is reciprocal too, so the pronoun is not optional.
9.4 «Un libro» — "Un libro" here means "one book" in contrast with the next one.
9.5 «te dice que empieces» — "Decir" in the sense of telling someone to do something requires the subjunctive: "que empieces". With the indicative it would simply report a fact.
9.6 «por la tarea más difícil» — "Empezar por" means "to start with" and takes "por", not "con". The superlative keeps the article: "la tarea más difícil".
9.7 «El siguiente» — "El siguiente" is an adjective used as a noun, with the article doing the work of English "one".
9.8 «insiste» — "Insistir en" takes "en", and here it introduces a clause: "insiste en que".
9.9 «en que primero hay que coger carrerilla» — "Hay que" plus the infinitive is the impersonal obligation, with no subject at all. "Coger carrerilla" is an idiom for building up momentum.
9.10 «Lo que de verdad me ha funcionado» — "Lo que" introduces a subject clause, "what". The present perfect marks a result that still holds.
9.11 «es mucho menos emocionante» — "Mucho menos emocionante" uses "mucho" to intensify and "menos" to build the comparison.
9.12 «menos pestañas y el despertador más temprano» — "Menos pestañas" needs no article. "El despertador más temprano" keeps its article, because the superlative always takes one.
9.13 «Nadie» — "Nadie" as the subject before the verb, so no second negative is needed.
9.14 «va a publicar» — "Ir a" plus the infinitive is the everyday future. Here it expresses a prediction about the world, not a plan.
9.15 «un libro sobre eso» — "Sobre eso" uses "sobre" for the topic. "Eso" is the neuter demonstrative and refers to the whole idea.
10.1 «Nos llevaba avisando» — "Llevar" plus a gerund in the imperfect: "nos llevaba avisando" means "she had been warning us". The indirect object pronoun comes first.
10.2 «de aquella fecha de entrega» — "Avisar de" takes "de". "Aquella" is the far demonstrative, more distant in time than "esa".
10.3 «desde hacía semanas» — "Desde hacía semanas" is the past counterpart of "desde hace": with a past tense the verb "hacer" also goes into the imperfect.
10.4 «Asentíamos» — "Asentir" is stem-changing: "asiento", "asientes", "asiente". The imperfect describes a repeated action.
10.5 «en cada reunión» — "En cada reunión" uses "cada" with a singular noun.
10.6 «y no cambiábamos nada» — "No cambiábamos nada" uses two negative words, which Spanish requires when "no" comes before the verb. English forbids exactly this.
10.7 «Cuando el cliente» — "Cuando" with the indicative here, because it refers to something that actually happened.
10.8 «adelantó la fecha» — "Adelantar" means to bring a date forward. The opposite would be "retrasar".
10.9 «el pánico fue casi impresionante» — "El pánico" keeps the article and carries a written accent. "Ser" is used for the assessment.
10.10 «De algún modo» — "De algún modo" means "somehow". "Algún" is the shortened form used before a masculine noun.
10.11 «lo entregamos» — "Lo" stands for the work delivered. The preterite places it at a definite moment.
10.12 «aquel viernes» — "Aquel viernes" uses the far demonstrative and takes no preposition, as days never do in Spanish.
10.13 «Ni una sola vez» — "Ni una sola vez" is an emphatic negation. Placed before the verb, it needs no additional "no".
10.14 «ha dicho «te lo dije»» — No inversion takes place after the fronted negative "ni una sola vez" — that is an English rule with no Spanish equivalent. Spanish uses angle quotes for direct speech inside a sentence.
10.15 «y por eso ahora todo el mundo la escucha» — "Por eso" introduces the conclusion. "La escucha" puts the feminine object pronoun before the verb, and "todo el mundo" takes a singular verb despite meaning "everyone".
