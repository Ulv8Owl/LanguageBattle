1.1 «Me levanto» — A reflexive verb, "levantarse": the pronoun "me" comes before the conjugated verb, not attached to the end. Spanish drops the subject pronoun, so "me levanto" already means "I get up" — adding "yo" would sound emphatic.
1.2 «a las siete» — Clock time takes "a las" with a feminine plural article, because "las horas" is understood: "a las siete", "a las dos". Only one o'clock is singular: "a la una".
1.3 «todas las mañanas» — Spanish uses the plural and keeps the article: "todas las mañanas", literally "all the mornings". English "every morning" is singular and article-free, which is why this one gets translated word for word by mistake.
1.4 «Luego preparo» — "Luego" means "then, afterwards". The ending "-o" carries "I", so no pronoun is needed. "Preparar" is the ordinary verb for making food and drink; "hacer" would be vaguer.
1.5 «café» — No article here, because this is an indefinite quantity — "some coffee". Say "el café" and you are talking about one specific coffee, not the drink in general.
1.6 «y leo las noticias» — The subject is not repeated, and Spanish would not repeat it anyway. "Las noticias" is always plural and always keeps its article, unlike English "the news".
2.1 «Mi oficina» — "Mi" has one form for both genders: "mi oficina", "mi hotel". It replaces the article, so "la mi oficina" is impossible.
2.2 «está cerca» — Location always uses "estar", never "ser": "está cerca". This is the split English does not have, and it is the classic mistake — "es cerca" is wrong here.
2.3 «de la estación de tren» — "Cerca" needs "de" before the place: "cerca de la estación". Spanish also builds compound nouns with "de": "estación de tren", never "tren estación".
2.4 «Voy» — "Ir" is irregular: "voy", "vas", "va". It means "go" in general; how you travel is added separately, in "a pie".
2.5 «allí» — "Allí" means "there" as a place. Spanish puts no preposition before it after a verb of motion: "voy allí", not "voy a allí".
2.6 «a pie en diez minutos» — "A pie" means "on foot" — Spanish uses a phrase where English has the single verb "walk". "En diez minutos" says how long the trip takes.
3.1 «No me gusta» — "Gustar" works backwards: the thing liked is the subject and the person is the indirect object. Literally "the cold is not pleasing to me", which is why it is "me gusta" and never "yo gusto".
3.2 «el frío» — Spanish needs the article when speaking about something in general: "el frío", "el café", "los perros". English drops it, and that missing "el" is the most common slip.
3.3 «así que me quedo en casa» — "Así que" means "so, therefore". "Quedarse" is reflexive, hence "me". "En casa" is a fixed phrase with no article: "en la casa" would mean one particular house.
3.4 «Hoy» — "Hoy" means "today". It is an adverb that can open the sentence, and it takes neither preposition nor article.
3.5 «hace» — Spanish describes weather with the verb "hacer": "hace viento", "hace frío", "hace calor" — literally "it makes wind". There is no subject at all, so both "es viento" and "está viento" are wrong.
3.6 «mucho viento» — Because "hacer" takes an object, the weather word is a noun, and the quantifier agrees with it: "mucho viento", masculine like "viento". English uses the adjective "windy" instead, which has no direct equivalent here.
4.1 «Mi hermano» — "Mi" again, unchanged by gender. The noun does the gender work: "hermano" is masculine, "hermana" feminine.
4.2 «trabaja» — Third person singular of the regular "-ar" verb "trabajar": "trabajo", "trabajas", "trabaja". The ending replaces the English pronoun.
4.3 «en un hotel grande» — "En" for being inside a place. The adjective normally follows the noun in Spanish: "un hotel grande", the opposite order from English.
4.4 «Empieza» — "Empezar" is stem-changing: "empiezo", "empiezas", "empieza" — the "e" becomes "ie" when stressed. The subject is understood from the ending.
4.5 «a trabajar» — After "empezar" Spanish requires "a" before the infinitive: "empieza a trabajar". The preposition is not optional, and English has nothing like it here.
4.6 «a las seis» — Clock time with "a las" again, exactly as in the first phrase.
5.1 «Tenemos» — "Tener" is irregular: "tengo", "tienes", "tiene", "tenemos". The "-mos" ending means "we", so no pronoun is needed.
5.2 «un perro pequeño» — "Un" because the dog is new to the conversation. The adjective goes after the noun and agrees with it: "un perro pequeño", "una casa pequeña".
5.3 «y duerme mucho» — "Dormir" is stem-changing: "duermo", "duermes", "duerme". The subject is still the dog, and Spanish simply leaves it out. "Mucho" follows the verb.
5.4 «Por la tarde» — Parts of the day take "por" plus the article: "por la mañana", "por la tarde", "por la noche". English "in the evening" uses a different preposition, and translating it as "en" is the usual mistake.
5.5 «paseamos» — "Pasear" means to walk for pleasure. The "-amos" ending marks "we", the same person as "tenemos" earlier.
5.6 «por el parque» — "Por" means movement around or through a place: "por el parque". "En el parque" would say you are inside it, without the idea of walking around.
6.1 «Soy» — "Ser" is used for identity and profession: "soy", "eres", "es". Using "estoy" here would be wrong — that verb is for states and locations.
6.2 «estudiante» — Spanish drops the article before a profession: "soy estudiante", not "soy un estudiante". This is the exact opposite of English, which requires "a".
6.3 «y estudio español» — "Estudio" is the first person of "estudiar". Language names are written in lower case in Spanish: "español", "inglés".
6.4 «Mi clase» — Singular "clase" where English might say "my class" or "my classes". "Mi" is the same for either gender.
6.5 «es» — "Ser" is used for when an event takes place: "la clase es los lunes". "Está" would be wrong here even though it feels like location — a scheduled event always takes "ser".
6.6 «los lunes y los jueves» — Repeated days take the plural article and no preposition: "los lunes" already means "on Mondays". Adding "en" is a common English-driven mistake.
7.1 «Esta tienda» — "Esta" is feminine to match "tienda"; with a masculine noun it would be "este". No article follows the demonstrative.
7.2 «está abierta» — "Estar" for a state that can change, and the adjective agrees with the feminine noun: "abierta". With "el bar" it would be "está abierto".
7.3 «todos los días» — Plural with the article, literally "all the days". Same pattern as "todas las mañanas" in the first phrase.
7.4 «Compro» — First person of "comprar". No pronoun needed, and no auxiliary: the Spanish present covers both "I buy" and "I am buying".
7.5 «pan y leche» — No article here, because these are indefinite quantities being bought. "El pan y la leche" would mean specific ones you have already mentioned.
7.6 «aquí» — "Aquí" means "here", with no preposition. It sits comfortably at the end of the sentence.
8.1 «Mi teléfono» — "Teléfono" is masculine, but "mi" does not change for gender — only for number, as in "mis teléfonos".
8.2 «es muy viejo» — "Ser" for a permanent characteristic: "es viejo". "Muy" goes before the adjective, and the adjective agrees in gender and number with "teléfono".
8.3 «así que va lento» — "Así que" means "so". "Ir" here means "to run, to work" when talking about devices: "va lento", literally "it goes slow".
8.4 «Quiero» — "Querer" is stem-changing: "quiero", "quieres", "quiere". Spanish has no continuous form for it, just as English has no "I am wanting".
8.5 «uno nuevo» — "Uno" stands in for the noun: "uno nuevo" is "a new one". Before a noun it would shorten to "un", but standing alone it keeps the full form.
8.6 «el año que viene» — Literally "the year that comes". Spanish keeps the article and needs no preposition: "en el año que viene" would be wrong here.
9.1 «Los sábados» — The plural article makes it habitual: "los sábados" means "on Saturdays". Days of the week are lower case in Spanish.
9.2 «limpio» — First person of "limpiar". Be careful: "limpio" is also the adjective "clean", and only the sentence tells them apart.
9.3 «mi habitación» — "Habitación" is feminine, but again "mi" does not change. It replaces the article completely.
9.4 «Después» — "Después" means "afterwards" and stands on its own. Add "de" only when something follows: "después de comer".
9.5 «cocino» — "Cocinar" is to cook with heat. Note that "cocina" as a noun means "kitchen" — the same root, a different word.
9.6 «la comida para mi familia» — "La comida" here is the midday meal, and Spanish keeps the article where English drops it before meals. "Para" expresses the person you cook for.
10.1 «No bebo» — Negation is a single "no" before the verb, with no auxiliary: "no bebo". English "I don't drink" needs "do"; Spanish does not.
10.2 «café» — No article, because "café" here is coffee in general. The same word as in the first phrase, and the same rule.
10.3 «por la noche» — Parts of the day with "por" and the article, like "por la tarde" above.
10.4 «Es malo» — "Ser" for a general judgement, and "malo" is masculine because it refers to the whole idea, not to any feminine noun. The subject is left unsaid.
10.5 «para mi sueño» — "Para" expresses purpose or the thing affected: "malo para". "Sueño" means both "sleep" and "dream" — here it is sleep.
10.6 «así que bebo té» — "Así que" introduces the result. "Té" carries an accent to tell it apart from "te", the pronoun.
