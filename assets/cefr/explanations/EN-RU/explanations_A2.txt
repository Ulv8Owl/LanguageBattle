1.1 «В прошлые выходные» — "Выходные" is plural only in this meaning, so "прошлые" is plural too. The preposition "в" plus the accusative marks a point in the past, and unlike English "last weekend", it cannot be dropped.
1.2 «мы навестили» — "Навестить" is perfective: the visit happened once and is over. Its imperfective partner "навещать" would describe a repeated habit. The ending marks a plural subject, agreeing with "мы".
1.3 «моих бабушку и дедушку» — Russian has no single word for "grandparents", so both are named. They are animate, and animate nouns take the genitive form in the accusative — hence "моих" rather than "мои".
1.4 «Они живут» — Present tense of "жить" with the plural ending: "они живут". Russian does not distinguish "they live" from "they are living"; one form covers both.
1.5 «в маленькой деревне» — "В" for location takes the prepositional case, and the adjective follows the noun in gender and case: "маленькая деревня" becomes "в маленькой деревне".
1.6 «у моря» — "У" always takes the genitive: "море" becomes "моря". It means "right by" — closer than "около", and with no idea of movement.
1.7 «Мы остались» — "Остаться" is perfective, a single completed stay. The past tense agrees with the subject in number and gender, never in person: "остались" serves any plural.
1.8 «там» — "Там" means "there" with no movement. "Туда" would mean "to there" and goes with verbs of motion.
1.9 «до вечера воскресенья» — "До" takes the genitive, so "вечер" becomes "вечера". The day is in the genitive too, "воскресенья", because it depends on "вечера" — literally "until the evening of Sunday".
2.1 «Я начал» — The Russian past tense agrees with the speaker's gender, not with the person: a man says "начал", a woman "начала". English has one form for everybody.
2.2 «новую работу» — Direct object in the accusative: "новая работа" becomes "новую работу". Both the adjective and the noun change their endings.
2.3 «в марте» — Months take "в" plus the prepositional case: "март" becomes "в марте". Month names are lower case in Russian.
2.4 «Офис» — The subject stays in the nominative. Russian has no articles at all, so nothing marks "офис" as "the office" — only the context does.
2.5 «намного больше» — "Больше" is the comparative of "большой" and is formed irregularly, not with a suffix. "Намного" means "much" and intensifies it.
2.6 «чем прежний» — "Чем" introduces the second half of a comparison. "Прежний" is an adjective standing on its own for "my old one": Russian needs no equivalent of English "one".
2.7 «Мне нравится» — "Нравиться" works backwards: the thing liked is the subject and the person goes into the dative, "мне". Literally "the team is pleasing to me", which is why there is no word for "I" in the nominative.
2.8 «моя команда» — Because it is the grammatical subject of "нравится", "команда" stays in the nominative, and "моя" agrees with it in the feminine.
2.9 «но рабочий день длинный» — There is no verb here at all: the present tense of "быть" is not used in Russian. "Длинный" is a full adjective agreeing with the masculine "день".
3.1 «Вчера» — An adverb, so no preposition and no ending. "Вчера" usually opens the sentence.
3.2 «я забыл зонт» — "Забыть" is perfective. The past "забыл" is masculine; a woman would say "забыла". "Зонт" is inanimate masculine, so its accusative looks like the nominative.
3.3 «дома» — A fixed adverb meaning "at home", stressed on the first syllable. It is not the same word as "дома", the plural of "дом", which is stressed on the second.
3.4 «Дождь шёл» — Russian gives the rain a real subject: "дождь" is the noun and "шёл" the verb — literally "the rain walked". There is no impersonal "it" as in English "it rained".
3.5 «после обеда» — "После" always takes the genitive, so "обед" becomes "обеда". The phrase means "in the afternoon", even though it looks like "after lunch".
3.6 «поэтому я сильно промок» — "Поэтому" means "that is why". "Промокнуть" is perfective, and its masculine past drops the suffix entirely: "промок", never "промокнул".
3.7 «В следующий раз» — "В" plus the accusative for a future point in time. Both words stay masculine accusative, which here happens to look like the nominative: "следующий раз".
3.8 «я посмотрю» — Perfective verbs have no present tense, so this form is the future: "посмотрю" means "I will check". Russian needs no auxiliary word like English "will".
3.9 «прогноз погоды заранее» — "Прогноз" is the accusative object and does not change form. "Погоды" is the genitive after it — the standard way Russian links two nouns where English simply puts them side by side.
4.1 «Мы едем» — "Ехать" is going by vehicle, as opposed to "идти" on foot. The present tense here describes a planned trip, exactly as English uses "we are going".
4.2 «в Португалию» — "В" plus the accusative marks direction; "в Португалии" with the prepositional would mean being there. Country names decline like ordinary nouns.
4.3 «в июле» — Months with "в" plus the prepositional: "июль" becomes "в июле", again with a lower-case month name.
4.4 «Моя жена» — "Моя" agrees with the feminine "жена", and the whole phrase is the subject, so it stays in the nominative.
4.5 «купила билеты» — "Купить" is perfective, a completed purchase. The ending of "купила" marks a female subject, agreeing with "жена". "Билеты" is accusative plural, identical to the nominative.
4.6 «на прошлой неделе» — Weeks take "на" plus the prepositional, not "в": "прошлая неделя" becomes "на прошлой неделе". Months and years take "в", so this preposition simply has to be memorised.
4.7 «Мне ещё нужно» — An impersonal construction: the person goes into the dative, "мне", and there is no grammatical subject at all. "Ещё" here means "still".
4.8 «купить» — After "нужно" the verb stays in the infinitive. The perfective "купить" presents the purchase as one completed act rather than a process.
4.9 «новый чемодан» — The accusative of an inanimate masculine noun looks exactly like the nominative, so neither word changes: "новый чемодан".
5.1 «Мой телефон» — "Мой" agrees with the masculine "телефон", and the phrase is the subject, so it is in the nominative.
5.2 «сломался» — The particle "-ся" makes the verb intransitive: the phone broke by itself. Without it, "сломал", somebody would have broken it.
5.3 «в прошлый вторник» — Days of the week take "в" plus the accusative: "в прошлый вторник". Day names are lower case in Russian.
5.4 «Я отнёс его» — "Отнести" is perfective and means carrying something away to a place. "Его" is the accusative of "он" and refers to the phone.
5.5 «в маленькую мастерскую» — Direction with "в" plus the accusative: "маленькая мастерская" becomes "в маленькую мастерскую". With the prepositional, "в мастерской", it would mean being there.
5.6 «рядом с офисом» — "Рядом" needs "с", and "с" takes the instrumental: "офис" becomes "офисом". On its own "рядом" cannot say what something is near.
5.7 «Ремонт» — The subject in the nominative. With no article, nothing marks "ремонт" as "the repair" — the previous sentence does that.
5.8 «был дешёвым» — In the past tense "быть" reappears, and the predicate adjective goes into the instrumental: "дешёвым". In the present there would be neither the verb nor that ending.
5.9 «но занял три дня» — "Занять" is perfective, "to take up time". After two, three and four the noun goes into the genitive singular: "три дня", never "три дни".
6.1 «Я обычно» — "Обычно" is an adverb of frequency and normally stands before the verb, just as English puts "usually" there.
6.2 «готовлю» — First person singular of "готовить". Note the "л" that appears only in this form: "готовлю", but "он готовит".
6.3 «дома» — The same fixed adverb as in the third phrase: "дома" means "at home" and takes no preposition.
6.4 «Рестораны» — The nominative plural as the subject. With no articles, the bare plural "рестораны" is how Russian says "restaurants in general".
6.5 «это приятно» — "Это" points back at the whole idea, and "приятно" is a neuter short form used impersonally. There is no verb and no real subject.
6.6 «но они дорогие» — "Они" replaces "рестораны". "Дорогие" is a full adjective in the nominative plural, and again there is no word for "are".
6.7 «В прошлом месяце» — Months take "в" plus the prepositional: "прошлый месяц" becomes "в прошлом месяце". A week would take "на" instead.
6.8 «я ел вне дома» — "Есть" is irregular: "я ем", past "ел". "Вне" takes the genitive, so "дом" becomes "дома" — the same letters as the adverb "дома" earlier, but a different word and a different stress.
6.9 «всего два раза» — "Всего" here means "only", not "in total". After "два" the noun takes the genitive singular: "два раза".
7.1 «Мой руководитель» — "Руководитель" is masculine, hence "мой". The word covers both "manager" and "head of department".
7.2 «попросил меня» — "Попросить" is perfective. "Меня" is the accusative of "я": the person asked goes straight into the accusative with no preposition, as in English.
7.3 «написать отчёт» — After "попросил меня" the verb stays in the infinitive; Russian has no separate word like English "to". "Написать" is perfective, so the report is meant to be finished.
7.4 «Я закончил» — Perfective past, masculine: "закончил". A woman would say "закончила".
7.5 «первую часть» — The accusative feminine: "первая часть" becomes "первую часть". Ordinal numbers are adjectives in Russian and agree like any others.
7.6 «вчера вечером» — Two adverbs in a row, the second in the instrumental: "вечером". Neither takes a preposition, and the order is fixed — day first, then part of the day.
7.7 «Я отправлю» — A perfective verb with present-tense endings means the future: "отправлю" is "I will send". No separate auxiliary is needed.
7.8 «остальное» — A neuter adjective used as a noun, "the rest". Russian lets an adjective stand alone like this with no noun after it.
7.9 «в пятницу» — Days take "в" plus the accusative: "пятница" becomes "в пятницу".
8.1 «Когда я был ребёнком» — After the past tense of "быть" the predicate noun goes into the instrumental: "ребёнком". In the present tense there would be no verb and the noun would stay in the nominative.
8.2 «я ненавидел» — Imperfective past: the feeling lasted over a period rather than happening once. The ending of "ненавидел" again depends on the speaker's gender.
8.3 «овощи» — The accusative plural, identical to the nominative for inanimate nouns. Russian uses the plural "овощи" here, just as English does.
8.4 «Теперь» — "Теперь" means "now" in contrast with an earlier state. "Сейчас" would mean "at this very moment" and would sound wrong here.
8.5 «я ем их» — "Есть" in the present: "я ем", "он ест". "Их" is the accusative of "они" and refers back to the vegetables.
8.6 «почти каждый день» — "Почти" stands directly before what it softens. "Каждый день" is a time expression in the accusative and takes no preposition.
8.7 «Люди» — "Люди" is the irregular plural of "человек". The form "человеки" does not exist, so the pair simply has to be learned.
8.8 «могут меняться» — After "мочь" the verb stays in the infinitive: "могут меняться". The "-ся" makes it intransitive — people change themselves, not something else.
8.9 «я думаю» — A softening phrase at the end of the sentence, exactly like English "I think", and separated by a comma.
9.1 «Спортзал» — The subject in the nominative. "Спортзал" is a compound of "спортивный" and "зал", and it is masculine.
9.2 «рядом с моим домом» — "Рядом с" plus the instrumental: "мой дом" becomes "моим домом". Both words take instrumental endings.
9.3 «закрылся в прошлом месяце» — The particle "-ся" again makes it intransitive: the gym closed, nobody closed it. "В прошлом месяце" uses "в" plus the prepositional, as in the sixth phrase.
9.4 «Теперь» — The same contrast with the past as in the eighth phrase: "теперь" sets the present against what used to be.
9.5 «я бегаю» — "Бегать" describes repeated or aimless running, while "бежать" would be one trip in one direction. A habit calls for "бегаю".
9.6 «в парке» — "В" plus the prepositional for being inside something: "парк" becomes "в парке".
9.7 «Это бесплатно» — "Бесплатно" is a neuter short form used impersonally, with no verb at all. "Это" refers back to running in the park.
9.8 «и воздух» — The nominative as the subject of what follows. With no article, nothing distinguishes "воздух" from "the air".
9.9 «намного лучше» — "Лучше" is the irregular comparative of "хороший". "Намного" is the same intensifier as in the second phrase.
10.1 «Я купил» — Perfective past: one completed purchase. The masculine ending again — a woman would say "купила".
10.2 «велосипед» — The accusative of an inanimate masculine noun, identical to the nominative: "велосипед".
10.3 «весной» — The instrumental with no preposition expresses a season: "весной", "летом", "зимой". It is the same pattern as "вечером" for times of day.
10.4 «Я пользуюсь им» — "Пользоваться" always takes the instrumental, so "он" becomes "им". This is a case the verb demands rather than one you could guess from the meaning.
10.5 «почти» — "Почти" softens what follows and stands directly before it, as in the eighth phrase.
10.6 «каждый день» — The accusative for a repeated time expression, with no preposition. "Каждый" is masculine because "день" is.
10.7 «Он быстрее» — "Он" refers to the bike: Russian pronouns follow grammatical gender, so an object can be "он". "Быстрее" is the comparative, and it needs no verb.
10.8 «автобуса» — After a comparative Russian can drop "чем" and put the second item in the genitive: "быстрее автобуса" means the same as "быстрее, чем автобус".
10.9 «и я лучше себя чувствую» — "Чувствовать себя" is a fixed pair for how one feels, and "себя" cannot be dropped. "Лучше" is the same irregular comparative as in the ninth phrase.
