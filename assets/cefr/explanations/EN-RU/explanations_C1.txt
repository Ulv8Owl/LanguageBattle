1.1 «Аттестация» — "Аттестация" is the standard Russian term for a formal performance review. It is the subject and stays in the nominative, with no article to mark it as definite.
1.2 «прошла примерно так же хорошо» — "Примерно так же хорошо" builds a comparison of equality: "так же" plus an adverb, with "примерно" softening it. All three words are needed; dropping "же" breaks the construction.
1.3 «как такие вещи вообще проходят» — "Как" opens the second half of the comparison, and the comma before it is compulsory. "Вообще" here means "generally, ever", not "in general" as a summary.
1.4 «Мне сообщили» — "Мне сообщили" is a subjectless plural verb: "they told me", with no "they" named. The person told goes into the dative. Russian uses this where English uses the passive "I was told".
1.5 «что у меня «отличные коммуникативные навыки»» — "У меня" plus the nominative is how Russian says "I have". The quotation is in guillemets, and the comma before "что" is compulsory.
1.6 «что, видимо, означает, что я отвечаю на письма» — Two subordinate clauses nested one inside the other, each with its own "что" and its own comma. "Видимо" is parenthetical and takes commas on both sides. "Отвечать на" takes the accusative.
1.7 «Ещё прозвучало» — "Прозвучало" literally means "sounded" and is used impersonally for something that was said. "Ещё" here means "also".
1.8 «мягкое пожелание» — The subject in the nominative, agreeing in the neuter: "мягкое пожелание". "Пожелание" is softer than "предложение" and carries a hint of an order.
1.9 «что мне стоит быть более стратегичным» — "Стоит" is an impersonal verb: the person goes into the dative, "мне", and an infinitive follows. "Более стратегичным" is in the instrumental, because "быть" in an infinitive takes that case.
1.10 «Никто до сих пор не объяснил» — "Никто не объяснил" needs both negative words. "До сих пор" means "so far, up to now" and does the work of the English perfect, which Russian does not have.
1.11 «что это слово значит» — An indirect question keeps the same word order as a direct one, and the comma before "что" is compulsory.
1.12 «на практике» — "На практике" is a fixed phrase with "на" plus the prepositional. It is the standard opposite of "в теории".
1.13 «Я кивал» — "Кивал" is imperfective past: the nodding went on for a while rather than happening once.
1.14 «так, как кивают тогда» — "Так, как" is a two-part construction with a compulsory comma between the halves. "Кивают" is a subjectless plural meaning "people nod", the equivalent of the English impersonal "you".
1.15 «когда альтернатива — разговор подольше» — A dash stands in for the missing verb "is" — the present tense of "быть" is never used, and the dash is how written Russian marks its absence. "Подольше" is a softened comparative: the prefix makes it "a bit longer".
1.16 «В целом» — "В целом" means "on balance, overall", with "в" plus the prepositional.
1.17 «я бы назвал это» — "Я бы назвал" is the conditional, built from the past tense plus the particle "бы". It softens the judgement exactly as English "I'd call it" does.
1.18 «продуктивными сорока минутами» — "Назвать" takes a second object in the instrumental: "продуктивными сорока минутами". Numerals decline in Russian, and "сорок" becomes "сорока" here — a form that surprises almost everyone.
2.1 «Я упёрся» — "Упереться в" plus the accusative means "to run up against". The particle "-ся" is part of the verb and cannot be dropped.
2.2 «в классическое плато» — "В классическое плато" is the accusative demanded by "упереться в". "Плато" is indeclinable and neuter, so only the adjective shows the case.
2.3 «где-то на B1» — "Где-то на B1" combines the vague "где-то" with "на" plus the prepositional. Russian marks a language level with "на", the same preposition as for a level or a floor.
2.4 «Всё, что я говорил» — "Всё, что" is a two-part frame, and the comma inside it is compulsory. "Всё" takes a singular verb.
2.5 «было формально правильным» — "Быть" in the past requires the predicate adjective in the instrumental: "правильным". In the present there would be no verb at all and the adjective would stand in the nominative.
2.6 «и совершенно безжизненным» — The second adjective keeps the same instrumental, because both depend on the same "было". Breaking that agreement would be a real mistake.
2.7 «Сдвинул дело с места» — "Сдвинуть дело с места" is a fixed idiom, "to get things moving". Putting the verb first is deliberate: Russian word order lets you delay the subject for effect.
2.8 «не учебник» — The negation attaches to the noun, not to the verb: "не учебник". This is the first half of the "не ... а ..." frame.
2.9 «а подкаст про футбол» — "А" is the contrastive conjunction that completes the frame, and it is not interchangeable with "и" or "но". "Про" is the colloquial equivalent of "о" and takes the accusative.
2.10 «Половину» — "Половину" is the accusative object, placed first for emphasis. Russian allows this freely because the ending, not the position, marks the role.
2.11 «я не понимал вообще» — "Не понимал вообще" is imperfective: the failure to understand went on throughout. "Вообще" here means "at all".
2.12 «и в этом, как выяснилось, был весь смысл» — "В этом" refers back to the whole preceding idea. "Как выяснилось" is a parenthetical clause with commas on both sides.
2.13 «Ритм языка» — "Ритм языка" links the two nouns with the genitive, which is Russian's standard way of joining them.
2.14 «ловишь гораздо раньше» — "Ловишь" is a second-person singular used impersonally, "one catches". This is the ordinary Russian equivalent of the English impersonal "you".
2.15 «чем словарный запас» — "Чем" introduces the second half of the comparison, with a compulsory comma before it.
2.16 «Теперь я звучу» — "Звучу" is the first person of "звучать". Unlike English "sound", it is not a linking verb here — what follows is a comparison, not a predicate adjective.
2.17 «почти как человек» — "Почти как человек" uses "как" plus the nominative for comparison. No preposition is involved.
2.18 «по крайней мере в удачные дни» — "По крайней мере" is a fixed phrase, and "в удачные дни" is "в" plus the accusative plural for a repeated occasion.
3.1 «Переезд в другой город» — "Переезд" is a verbal noun where English uses a gerund. "В другой город" is "в" plus the accusative for direction.
3.2 «в тридцать с чем-то» — "В тридцать с чем-то" uses "в" plus the accusative for age, and "с чем-то" means "-something". The phrase is colloquial and lively.
3.3 «это странная форма перезагрузки» — The dash stands for the missing "is". "Форма перезагрузки" links the nouns with the genitive.
3.4 «Проблемы» — The subject in the nominative plural: nothing marks "проблемы" as "the problems" — Russian has no articles at all.
3.5 «остаются те же» — "Те же" means "the same ones" and agrees with the plural subject. "Остаются" has "-ся" and is intransitive.
3.6 «меняются только стены» — "Только" limits what follows and stands directly before it. "Меняются" again has "-ся": the walls change by themselves.
3.7 «Первые два месяца» — "Первые два месяца" is the accusative of duration, with no preposition. Note the word order: the ordinal comes before the cardinal, as in English.
3.8 «я был уверен» — "Был уверен" uses the past of "быть" with a short-form adjective. In the present the verb would disappear entirely.
3.9 «что совершил ошибку» — "Совершить ошибку" is the fixed collocation; "сделать ошибку" is possible but weaker. The comma before "что" is compulsory.
3.10 «Потом бариста» — "Потом" means "then, after that". "Бариста" is indeclinable and here masculine, though the word can be used for either gender.
3.11 «начал помнить мой заказ» — After "начал" the verb stays in the infinitive, and the imperfective "помнить" describes a lasting state rather than a single act.
3.12 «и что-то тихо встало на место» — "Встать на место" is an idiom, "to fall into place". "Что-то" is singular and takes a neuter past-tense verb.
3.13 «Выходит» — "Выходит" is used impersonally, "it turns out". The comma after it is optional and here left out for pace.
3.14 «ощущение своего места» — "Ощущение своего места" links three words with the genitive. "Свой" refers back to the implied subject, which is what makes the phrase mean "belonging".
3.15 «это в основном повторение» — The dash again replaces the missing "is". "В основном" means "mostly", and "повторение" is a verbal noun.
3.16 «Я всё ещё скучаю» — "Всё ещё" means "still" in the sense of continuing, and it is not the same as "и всё же", which marks contrast.
3.17 «по старой квартире» — "Скучать по" takes the dative: "по квартире". This government has to be memorised with the verb.
3.18 «главным образом по свету» — "Главным образом" is a fixed instrumental phrase, "mainly". The second "по" is repeated because the construction requires it.
4.1 «Мы попросили у него» — "Попросить у кого-то что-то" puts the person in the genitive after "у" and the thing in the accusative. English uses "ask somebody for something", so both halves differ.
4.2 «простую форму» — "Простую форму" is the accusative feminine, with both words taking those endings.
4.3 «желательно к четвергу» — "Желательно" is an impersonal predicate, "ideally". "К четвергу" uses "к" plus the dative for a deadline — a case English marks with the preposition "by".
4.4 «То, что вернулось» — "То, что" is a two-part frame with a compulsory comma. It builds a subject clause, "what came back".
4.5 «через три недели» — "Через три недели" uses "через" plus the accusative for a period after which something happens.
4.6 «было маленьким прекрасным фреймворком» — After the past of "быть" the predicate noun goes into the instrumental: "фреймворком", with both adjectives following suit.
4.7 «Он обрабатывал» — "Обрабатывал" is imperfective: the framework handled those cases as a matter of course, not once.
4.8 «все случаи» — "Все случаи" is the accusative plural, identical to the nominative for inanimate nouns.
4.9 «о которых мы ни разу не заикались» — "О которых" places the preposition before the relative pronoun, and it takes the prepositional case. "Ни разу не заикались" needs both negative words, and "заикаться о" is an idiom meaning "to breathe a word about".
4.10 «Если совсем честно» — "Если совсем честно" is an elliptical parenthetical phrase with the verb left out. The comma after it is compulsory.
4.11 «код» — The subject in the nominative. Note the lower-case letter: "код" opens a clause after a comma, not a new sentence.
4.12 «действительно элегантный» — A full adjective as predicate, with no verb: the present of "быть" is not used. "Действительно" means "genuinely".
4.13 «И он же» — "Он же" uses the particle "же" for emphasis: "and it is also the very thing". This particle has no direct English equivalent.
4.14 «единственное» — A neuter adjective used as a noun, "the only thing". Russian lets adjectives stand alone like this.
4.15 «что никто другой не сможет поддерживать» — "Никто другой не сможет" needs both negative words. "Поддерживать" is imperfective, because maintenance is a continuing activity.
4.16 «Здесь есть урок» — "Здесь есть" states existence. "Урок" is the subject in the nominative.
4.17 «который я сам» — "Который" is the relative pronoun in the accusative here, matching its role in its own clause. "Сам" emphasises the subject and agrees with it.
4.18 «никак не выучу» — "Никак не выучу" needs both negatives, and "никак" adds "no matter how I try". The perfective future is used because the learning is imagined as a completed act that never comes.
5.1 «У моего отца» — "У моего отца" plus the nominative is the Russian "have" construction. The owner goes into the genitive after "у".
5.2 «сложились» — "Сложиться" has "-ся" and means "to take shape by itself" — no one made the relationship complicated.
5.3 «непростые отношения с планшетом» — "Отношения с" takes the instrumental: "с планшетом". "Непростой" is a common euphemism for "difficult".
5.4 «Каждое уведомление» — "Каждое уведомление" is the accusative object placed first for emphasis, and "каждый" agrees with the neuter noun.
5.5 «он воспринимает как личный вызов» — "Воспринимать как" takes the accusative after "как", so the case is copied from the object.
5.6 «своему авторитету» — "Своему авторитету" is the dative demanded by "вызов". "Свой" points back to the subject of the sentence, not to the speaker.
5.7 «Когда я приезжаю» — "Когда я приезжаю" uses the imperfective present for something that happens regularly. Russian would use the future only if a single visit were meant.
5.8 «мы двадцать минут» — "Двадцать минут" is the accusative of duration with no preposition.
5.9 «решаем кризисы, которые я не могу воспроизвести» — "Решаем кризисы" is imperfective present, and the relative clause takes a compulsory comma. "Не могу воспроизвести" places the negation before the modal, not before the infinitive.
5.10 «В прошлом месяце» — "В прошлом месяце" uses "в" plus the prepositional.
5.11 «он прислал мне голосовое» — "Прислать" takes the recipient in the dative and the thing in the accusative. "Голосовое" is an adjective used as a noun, short for "голосовое сообщение".
5.12 «длиной в четыре секунды дыхания» — "Длиной в" plus the accusative expresses extent. After "четыре" the noun goes into the genitive singular, and "дыхания" is a further genitive.
5.13 «Я перестал объяснять» — After "перестать" the verb must be imperfective: "объяснять". A perfective here would be ungrammatical, because you cannot stop doing something that is thought of as complete.
5.14 «и начал» — The second verb shares the subject, so "я" is not repeated. "И начал" simply continues the same clause.
5.15 «просто чинить» — After "начал" the verb is again imperfective, and "просто" stands before it.
5.16 «Ему спокойнее» — "Ему спокойнее" is impersonal: the person goes into the dative and a comparative adverb follows, with no verb at all.
5.17 «мне быстрее» — The same impersonal frame as "ему спокойнее", repeated for balance: dative plus a comparative adverb, with no verb. The parallelism is deliberate.
5.18 «и никому не приходится ни в чём признаваться» — "Никому не приходится ни в чём признаваться" stacks three negative words, and all of them are compulsory. "Приходится" is impersonal, "one has to", with the person in the dative.
6.1 «Воркшоп» — "Воркшоп" is a recent borrowing and declines like any masculine noun. Here it is the accusative object placed first.
6.2 «анонсировали» — "Анонсировали" is a subjectless plural: "they announced it". Russian prefers this where English uses the passive.
6.3 «как сессию стратегической синхронизации» — "Как" plus the accusative for what something was billed as. "Сессию стратегической синхронизации" chains two genitives after the object.
6.4 «На практике» — "На практике" is the fixed phrase from the first phrase.
6.5 «это были одиннадцать человек» — "Это были" uses a plural verb agreeing with "одиннадцать человек". After numerals ending in "-надцать" the noun takes the genitive plural, and "человек" has a zero ending there.
6.6 «громко соглашавшихся ни о чём» — "Соглашавшихся" is an active past participle agreeing with "человек" in the genitive plural. Participles like this are typical of written Russian and rare in speech.
6.7 «Кто-то вынес» — "Кто-то" is singular and takes a masculine past-tense verb, whatever the actual person.
6.8 «слово «синергия»» — "Слово «синергия»" keeps the quoted word in the nominative, because it is named rather than used. Russian uses guillemets.
6.9 «на настоящий слайд» — "На настоящий слайд" is "на" plus the accusative for direction. "Настоящий" means "real, actual", not "present-day".
6.10 «Хотелось бы сказать» — "Хотелось бы" is an impersonal conditional: the person would go into the dative, and here it is left out entirely.
6.11 «что я возразил» — The comma before "что" is compulsory. "Возразить" is perfective, a single act of objecting.
6.12 «но я в основном смотрел на часы» — "Смотреть на" takes the accusative. "В основном" means "mostly".
6.13 «Мы разошлись» — "Разойтись" has "-ся" and means "to disperse, to go separate ways".
6.14 «с четырьмя пунктами» — "С четырьмя пунктами" is "с" plus the instrumental, and the numeral declines: "четыре" becomes "четырьмя".
6.15 «у двух из которых были ответственные» — "У двух из которых" combines "у" plus the genitive with a relative pronoun, and the numeral declines again. "Были ответственные" states existence in the past.
6.16 «К пятнице» — "К пятнице" uses "к" plus the dative for a deadline, as in the fourth phrase.
6.17 «всё это» — "Всё это" is the subject, neuter singular.
6.18 «тихо испарилось» — "Испариться" has "-ся" and is used figuratively. "Тихо" stands before the verb.
7.1 «У меня был» — "У меня был" is the "have" construction in the past, and the verb agrees with the thing owned: "был" for the masculine "период".
7.2 «период» — The subject in the nominative: "период" carries no article, and only the context makes it "a phase".
7.3 «когда я относился к хлебу очень серьёзно» — "Когда" opens a relative clause of time with a compulsory comma. "Относиться к" takes the dative: "к хлебу".
7.4 «В холодильнике жила закваска» — "Жила" is used figuratively of the starter, exactly as English might say "lived". The subject follows the verb here, which Russian word order allows freely.
7.5 «с именем» — "С именем" is "с" plus the instrumental.
7.6 «и графиком кормления» — The preposition "с" carries over to the second noun without being repeated, and "графиком" stays in the instrumental. "Кормления" is a genitive linking the nouns.
7.7 «Недель шесть» — "Недель шесть" reverses the usual order to mean "about six weeks". This inversion is the standard colloquial way of expressing approximation.
7.8 «на кухне» — "На кухне" takes "на", not "в" — one of the fixed choices Russian makes for certain places.
7.9 «пахло по-настоящему прекрасно» — "Пахло" is impersonal: there is no subject at all, and the verb is in the neuter. "По-настоящему" is written with a hyphen.
7.10 «Потом навалилась работа» — "Навалиться" is a vivid perfective, "to pile on". The subject follows the verb, which suits the sudden sense.
7.11 «и закваска» — The subject in the nominative, already known from the fourth element. "И закваска" simply resumes it after "и".
7.12 «тихо скончалась» — "Скончаться" is a formal word for dying, used here ironically about a sourdough starter. The irony is the point, and a neutral verb would lose it.
7.13 «Я говорю людям» — "Говорить людям" puts the person in the dative. The imperfective describes something said repeatedly.
7.14 «что вернусь к этому» — "Вернуться к" takes the dative: "к этому". The perfective future needs no auxiliary word.
7.15 «когда станет поспокойнее» — "Когда станет" uses the future in the subordinate clause, unlike English. "Поспокойнее" is a doubly softened comparative: both the prefix and the suffix reduce the force.
7.16 «Мы оба знаем» — "Мы оба" places "оба" after the pronoun. It agrees in gender: "обе" would be used for two women.
7.17 «что спокойнее» — The comma before "что" is compulsory.
7.18 «не становится» — "Не становится" is imperfective present with the negation before the verb. The comparative "спокойнее" stands as the predicate with no noun at all.
8.1 «Мы знакомы» — "Мы знакомы" uses a short-form adjective as the predicate, with no verb: "we are acquainted".
8.2 «достаточно давно» — "Достаточно давно" places "достаточно" before the adverb, whereas English puts "enough" after it.
8.3 «чтобы пропускать сводку новостей» — "Чтобы" plus an infinitive expresses purpose, and the comma before it is compulsory. "Сводка новостей" links the nouns with the genitive.
8.4 «Он звонит» — "Звонит" is imperfective present for a habit.
8.5 «раз в несколько месяцев» — "Раз в несколько месяцев" uses "в" plus the accusative for frequency. After "несколько" the noun goes into the genitive plural.
8.6 «обычно за рулём» — "За рулём" is a fixed instrumental phrase, "at the wheel". Russian says it with no verb at all, where English needs "while driving".
8.7 «Ничего важного» — "Ничего важного" puts both words in the genitive: after a negation the object regularly shifts to that case, and the adjective follows the pronoun.
8.8 «не говорится» — "Не говорится" combines the negation with "-ся", giving a passive with no agent.
8.9 «в первые десять минут» — "В первые десять минут" is "в" plus the accusative, with the ordinal before the cardinal.
8.10 «Потом, ближе к концу» — "Ближе к концу" is a comparative adverb plus "к" with the dative.
8.11 «одна настоящая вещь» — "Одна настоящая вещь" is the subject in the nominative. "Одна" here means "some one", not the number.
8.12 «всё-таки проскакивает» — "Всё-таки" is written with a hyphen and means "after all, nonetheless". "Проскакивает" is imperfective, for something that happens each time.
8.13 «Никто из нас» — "Никто из нас" uses "из" plus the genitive to pick out of a group, and it will require "не" with the verb.
8.14 «это не комментирует» — "Это не комментирует" completes the double negation. "Комментировать" takes a direct object with no preposition, unlike English "comment on".
8.15 «и в этом вся договорённость» — "В этом вся договорённость" has no verb: the present of "быть" is absent, and "вся" agrees with the feminine noun.
8.16 «Мне было бы трудно» — "Мне было бы трудно" is an impersonal conditional: the person in the dative, the past of "быть", the particle "бы" and a short-form adverb.
8.17 «объяснить такую дружбу» — The infinitive follows the impersonal predicate. "Такую дружбу" is the accusative feminine.
8.18 «кому-то младше тридцати» — "Кому-то младше тридцати" puts the person in the dative, as "объяснить" requires, and the comparative "младше" takes the genitive after it.
9.1 «Чат-бот» — "Чат-бот" is written with a hyphen and declines like any masculine noun.
9.2 «сегодня очень рвался» — "Рваться" has "-ся" and means "to be eager", literally "to tear oneself". "Очень" intensifies it.
9.3 «мне помочь» — "Мне помочь" puts the person in the dative, because "помочь" always governs that case. The infinitive follows the conjugated verb.
9.4 «Он спросил» — "Спросил" is perfective, a single act of asking.
9.5 «не пробовал ли я» — "Не пробовал ли я" builds an indirect question with the particle "ли". The negation here is not a real negation: it makes the question tentative and polite, a nuance English marks with intonation.
9.6 «выключить и включить роутер» — Two perfective infinitives sharing one object. Russian says "выключить и включить" in that order, the reverse of English "off and on" — a small fixed difference.
9.7 «Пробовал» — A one-word answer, "пробовал", consisting of the verb alone. Russian repeats the verb where English repeats the auxiliary, because it has no auxiliary to repeat.
9.8 «дважды» — "Дважды" is the bookish form; "два раза" is the everyday one. Both are correct here.
9.9 «о чём уже написал ему текстом» — "О чём" places the preposition before the relative pronoun, which takes the prepositional. "Текстом" is an instrumental of means, "in writing".
9.10 «Через одиннадцать минут» — "Через одиннадцать минут" uses "через" plus the accusative, with the noun in the genitive plural after the numeral.
9.11 «он предложил» — "Предложил" is perfective and takes an infinitive after it.
9.12 «соединить меня с человеком» — "Соединить с" takes the instrumental. "Меня" is the accusative of "я".
9.13 «Человек» — The subject in the nominative: "человек" is the person just mentioned, and no article marks that.
9.14 «задал тот же вопрос» — "Тот же" means "the same" and agrees with the masculine accusative "вопрос". "Задать вопрос" is the fixed collocation.
9.15 «но хотя бы звучал смущённо» — "Хотя бы" means "at least". "Звучал" here takes an adverb, "смущённо", where English would use the adjective "embarrassed" after a linking verb — a genuine difference in construction.
9.16 «Прогресс» — The subject in the nominative. "Прогресс" is masculine and takes no article.
9.17 «в некотором роде» — "В некотором роде" is a fixed phrase with "в" plus the prepositional, "of a sort".
9.18 «и роутер теперь работает» — "Теперь" contrasts with the earlier state, and "работает" is imperfective present.
10.1 «Я ушёл со стабильной работы» — "Уйти с" plus the genitive means "to leave a job". Note that "работа" pairs with "с", while a building would pair with "из".
10.2 «ради того» — "Ради того" plus the genitive introduces what follows, and "того" is the pointing word that the clause needs.
10.3 «что заметно менее надёжно» — "Что заметно менее надёжно" uses the neuter short form as the predicate. "Менее" builds the comparative for words that do not take a suffix.
10.4 «Все говорили» — "Все" is plural in Russian and takes a plural verb.
10.5 «что это смело» — "Это смело" has no verb, and "смело" is a neuter short form used impersonally.
10.6 «а так говорят, когда имеют в виду «безрассудно»» — "А" contrasts with what came before. "Иметь в виду" is a fixed three-word idiom that cannot be broken up.
10.7 «Первый год» — "Первый год" is the nominative subject, with the ordinal agreeing.
10.8 «дался по-настоящему тяжело» — "Даться" has "-ся" and is impersonal in feel: the year "gave itself" hard. The person would go into the dative.
10.9 «и я не советую это делать легкомысленно» — "Не советую" places the negation before the verb, and "легкомысленно" is an adverb at the end.
10.10 «Но я перестал» — "Но я перестал" marks the turn.
10.11 «смотреть на часы» — After "перестать" the verb must be imperfective: "смотреть". "Смотреть на" takes the accusative.
10.12 «в четыре дня» — "В четыре дня" uses "в" plus the accusative for the hour, and "дня" is the genitive marking the part of the day. Russian needs no separate word for "afternoon".
10.13 «На бумаге» — "На бумаге" is "на" plus the prepositional, a fixed phrase meaning "written down".
10.14 «это звучит мелко» — "Это звучит мелко" takes an adverb after "звучит", not an adjective — the opposite of the English rule for linking verbs.
10.15 «и это не так» — "Это не так" is a compact denial with no verb: literally "this is not so".
10.16 «Спросите меня снова» — The imperative plural "спросите", used both for several people and as the polite form for one. "Снова" goes at the end.
10.17 «лет через пять» — "Лет через пять" reverses the usual order to mean "in about five years", the same inversion as "недель шесть" earlier.
10.18 «и, может быть, у меня будет нормальный ответ» — "У меня будет ответ" is the "have" construction in the future, and the verb agrees with "ответ". "Может быть" is parenthetical and takes commas.
