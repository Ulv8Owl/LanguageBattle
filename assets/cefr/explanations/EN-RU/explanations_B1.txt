1.1 «Я работаю» — Russian has no perfect tenses: an action that started earlier and is still going on is simply the present, "я работаю". The English "I've been working" has no separate form here — the duration is carried by the words that follow, not by the verb.
1.2 «в одной и той же команде» — "Один и тот же" is a fixed three-word phrase for "the same", and both parts decline: here the prepositional after "в" gives "в одной и той же". Saying "в тот же команде" would leave the agreement broken.
1.3 «уже три года» — "Уже" plus the plain accusative expresses duration up to now: "уже три года". There is no preposition at all, so the English "for" has nothing to translate into.
1.4 «В последнее время» — "В последнее время" is a fixed phrase in the prepositional case, literally "in the last time", meaning "recently". It is one of the very few time expressions that keeps "в".
1.5 «я начал» — The past of "начать", masculine. Russian marks the speaker's gender here, not the person: a woman would say "начала".
1.6 «чувствовать, что застрял» — After "начал" the second verb stays in the infinitive: "начал чувствовать". And where English says "to feel stuck" with an adjective, Russian opens a subordinate clause with "что" and a full verb — there is no way to attach a participle directly.
1.7 «Руководитель» — The subject in the nominative. Russian often drops the possessive where English needs one: "руководитель" alone is understood as "my manager".
1.8 «предложил короткий курс» — "Предложить" is perfective, a single completed act. "Короткий курс" is the accusative of an inanimate masculine noun, so neither word changes its ending.
1.9 «и на прошлой неделе я записался» — "На прошлой неделе" takes "на", not "в" — weeks are the exception. "Записался" carries the particle "-ся", which makes it "signed myself up".
1.10 «Если всё пойдёт хорошо» — In an "если" clause Russian does use the future, unlike English: "если всё пойдёт хорошо". A present tense here would change the meaning to a repeated condition.
1.11 «я, возможно, подам заявку» — "Возможно" is set off by commas and can be dropped without breaking the sentence — that is exactly why it takes them. "Подам" is a perfective future: no auxiliary word is needed.
1.12 «на другую должность» — "Подать заявку на что-то" takes "на" plus the accusative: "на другую должность". Both words are feminine accusative, so both change their endings.
2.1 «Мы переехали» — "Переехать" is perfective and specifically means moving house. The plural past "переехали" agrees with "мы" in number, with no marking for person.
2.2 «в новую квартиру» — Direction with "в" plus the accusative: "новая квартира" becomes "в новую квартиру". With the prepositional, "в новой квартире", it would mean already living there.
2.3 «в апреле» — Months take "в" plus the prepositional: "апрель" becomes "в апреле".
2.4 «Дом» — The subject in the nominative. "Дом" here means the building, not the home — that meaning belongs to the adverb "дома".
2.5 «старый» — A full adjective as predicate, with no verb: the present tense of "быть" is not used in Russian. "Старый" agrees with the masculine "дом".
2.6 «поэтому что-то ломается довольно часто» — "Что-то" is an indefinite pronoun, "something", and takes a singular verb. "Ломается" has "-ся", so things break by themselves. "Довольно часто" means "quite often".
2.7 «В прошлом месяце» — Months take "в" plus the prepositional: "прошлый месяц" becomes "в прошлом месяце".
2.8 «отопление» — "Отопление" is a neuter noun in the nominative, the subject of what follows. Russian has no article, so nothing marks it as "the heating".
2.9 «не работало целую неделю» — The imperfective past for a state that lasted: "не работало", neuter to agree with "отопление". "Целую неделю" is the accusative of duration, with no preposition.
2.10 «Хозяин квартиры» — "Хозяин квартиры" links two nouns with the genitive, "квартиры", where English uses a single word "landlord". This genitive link is Russian's standard way of joining nouns.
2.11 «в итоге всё починил» — "В итоге" means "in the end". "Починил" is perfective, so the repair was completed; "чинил" would say he was working on it without saying he finished.
2.12 «но это заняло слишком много времени» — "Занять" is perfective, "to take up time". "Слишком много времени" puts the noun in the genitive after "много" — a quantity word always demands it.
3.1 «Я учу испанский» — The present tense with no perfect: "я учу испанский" covers "I have been learning". "Учить" means to study a subject; "изучать" is its more formal partner.
3.2 «примерно год» — "Примерно" means "about". The duration goes in the plain accusative with no preposition: "год".
3.3 «в основном в телефоне» — "В основном" is a fixed phrase meaning "mostly". Russian says "в телефоне", inside the phone, where English says "on my phone" — the preposition is genuinely different.
3.4 «Я могу читать» — After "мочь" the verb stays in the infinitive: "могу читать". "Читать" is imperfective, describing the ability rather than one completed act.
3.5 «меню и короткие статьи» — "Меню" is indeclinable and neuter, so it never changes. "Статьи" is the accusative plural, identical to the nominative for inanimate nouns, with the adjective agreeing.
3.6 «без особых сложностей» — "Без" always takes the genitive: "особые сложности" becomes "особых сложностей". Note that the genitive plural of this noun has no ending of its own.
3.7 «Говорить» — An infinitive used as the subject: "говорить" here means "speaking". Russian has no gerund for this, so the infinitive does the job.
3.8 «вот что до сих пор трудно» — "Вот что" points forward and adds emphasis, roughly "that is what". "Трудно" is a neuter short form used impersonally, with no verb at all.
3.9 «особенно по телефону» — "Особенно" means "especially". "По телефону" uses "по" plus the dative — a fixed way of saying "by phone", not translatable preposition by preposition.
3.10 «Следующим летом» — The instrumental with no preposition for a season: "следующим летом", like "весной" and "вечером". Both words take instrumental endings.
3.11 «я хотел бы провести» — "Хотел бы" is the conditional, formed with the past tense plus the particle "бы". It softens "хочу" exactly as English "would like" softens "want".
3.12 «две недели в Валенсии» — "Провести" is perfective, "to spend time". "Две недели" is the accusative of duration, and after "две" the noun takes the genitive singular. The city takes "в" plus the prepositional: "в Валенсии".
4.1 «Наш рейс» — "Наш" agrees with the masculine "рейс", and the whole phrase is the object of the next verb, though its form does not change.
4.2 «задержали» — Russian uses a subjectless plural verb where English uses the passive: "задержали" means "they delayed it", with no "they" named. "Рейс" is the accusative object, identical to the nominative.
4.3 «на четыре часа» — "На" plus the accusative expresses the size of a difference: "на четыре часа". After "четыре" the noun takes the genitive singular, "часа".
4.4 «Никто» — "Никто" is a negative pronoun, and Russian requires the verb to be negated as well: "никто не смог". This double negation is compulsory, the exact opposite of the English rule.
4.5 «на стойке» — "На стойке" uses "на" plus the prepositional for a service point. The choice between "в" and "на" here is fixed by usage and simply has to be learned.
4.6 «не смог объяснить почему» — "Смог" is the perfective past of "мочь", and the negation "не" stands before it, matching "никто". After it the infinitive: "объяснить почему".
4.7 «В итоге» — "В итоге" again, "in the end".
4.8 «нам выдали» — Another subjectless plural: "нам выдали" is "they gave us". The recipient goes into the dative, "нам", and the thing given into the accusative.
4.9 «талон на бутерброд» — "Талон на бутерброд" uses "на" plus the accusative for what the voucher is for. Russian has no articles, so nothing marks either noun as definite.
4.10 «Я решил» — "Решить" is perfective, the decision made once. The masculine past again depends on the speaker's gender.
4.11 «лететь другой авиакомпанией» — After "решил" the verb stays in the infinitive: "лететь". "Другой авиакомпанией" is in the instrumental — the case Russian uses for the means of doing something.
4.12 «в следующий раз» — "В следующий раз" takes "в" plus the accusative, unlike English "next time", which needs no preposition at all.
5.1 «Я когда-то играл» — "Когда-то" means "at one time, once". The imperfective past "играл" describes a lasting habit, which is how Russian expresses English "used to play" — there is no separate construction for it.
5.2 «на гитаре» — Playing an instrument uses "на" plus the prepositional: "на гитаре", "на пианино". Playing a game would use "в" plus the accusative: "в футбол".
5.3 «когда учился в школе» — "Учиться" has "-ся" and means to be a student. "В школе" uses "в" plus the prepositional.
5.4 «Я это бросил» — "Бросить" is perfective, "to give up". "Это" is the accusative object and refers back to playing the guitar.
5.5 «когда начал работать» — After "начал" the verb stays in the infinitive: "начал работать", imperfective, because the work is a continuing state.
5.6 «и потом жалел» — "Потом" means "afterwards". "Жалел" is imperfective past: the regret lasted rather than happening once.
5.7 «Мой друг» — "Мой друг" in the nominative as the subject. Russian says "друг" for a close friend; a mere acquaintance would be "знакомый".
5.8 «одолжил мне свою старую» — "Одолжить" takes the recipient in the dative, "мне", and the thing in the accusative. "Свою старую" is a feminine adjective standing alone for "his old guitar" — Russian needs no equivalent of English "one".
5.9 «в январе» — Months with "в" plus the prepositional: "январь" becomes "в январе".
5.10 «Теперь» — "Теперь" contrasts the present with an earlier state, whereas "сейчас" would mean "at this very moment".
5.11 «я занимаюсь» — "Заниматься" always takes the instrumental when it has an object, but here it stands alone and means "to practise, to study".
5.12 «по двадцать минут каждый вечер» — "По" plus the accusative distributes a quantity: "по двадцать минут" means twenty minutes each time. "Каждый вечер" is the accusative of a repeated time, with no preposition.
6.1 «Моя сестра» — "Моя" agrees with the feminine "сестра", and the phrase is the subject, so it stays in the nominative.
6.2 «только что закончила» — "Только что" means "just now" and goes with a perfective past. Russian has no perfect tense, so the adverb carries all the meaning that English puts into "has just".
6.3 «первый курс университета» — "Первый курс университета" links two nouns with the genitive: "университета". And "курс" means an academic year, not a course of lectures — that would be "курс лекций".
6.4 «Она изучает» — "Изучает" is imperfective present, for an ongoing course of study. Russian makes no distinction between "she studies" and "she is studying".
6.5 «что-то связанное с данными» — "Что-то связанное с данными" uses a participle plus "с" with the instrumental. "Данные" is plural only, and its instrumental is "данными".
6.6 «чего я толком не понимаю» — "Чего" is the genitive of "что", required because the verb is negated: after a negative verb the object often shifts from accusative to genitive. "Толком" means "properly, really".
6.7 «Каждый раз, когда я спрашиваю» — "Каждый раз, когда" is the standard rendering of English "whenever", and the comma before "когда" is compulsory.
6.8 «она объясняет» — "Объяснять" takes the thing in the accusative and the person in the dative, so "мне" could be added freely. Here the object is left out because it is obvious.
6.9 «очень терпеливо» — "Терпеливо" is an adverb formed from "терпеливый". "Очень" intensifies it and stands directly before.
6.10 «Я по-прежнему» — "По-прежнему" means "still, as before" and is written with a hyphen. It is more emphatic than a plain "ещё".
6.11 «ничего не понимаю» — "Ничего не понимаю" has the compulsory double negation again: "ничего" and "не" must both be present. "Ничего" is the genitive form used after a negated verb.
6.12 «но всё равно горжусь ею» — "Всё равно" means "anyway, all the same". "Гордиться" always takes the instrumental: "ею", the instrumental of "она".
7.1 «Мы должны были запустить» — "Должны были" plus the infinitive means an obligation that existed in the past; like English "were supposed to", it hints that it did not happen. "Запустить" is perfective.
7.2 «новый сайт» — The accusative of an inanimate masculine noun, unchanged in form: "новый сайт".
7.3 «в июне» — Months with "в" plus the prepositional: "июнь" becomes "в июне".
7.4 «Потом дизайнер» — "Потом" means "then, after that". "Дизайнер" is the subject in the nominative.
7.5 «ушёл из компании» — "Уйти из" plus the genitive means leaving an organisation: "из компании". The pair "в" and "из" work together, so a place you go "в" you leave "из".
7.6 «и всё встало на месяц» — "Всё" here means "everything" and takes a singular verb. "Встало" is a perfective past of "встать" used figuratively, "came to a halt". "На месяц" uses "на" plus the accusative for an intended period.
7.7 «Мы наконец» — "Наконец" means "finally" and needs no commas in this position.
7.8 «выкатили его» — "Выкатить" is a colloquial perfective for releasing a product. "Его" is the accusative of "он", referring to the site.
7.9 «в прошлый четверг» — Days of the week take "в" plus the accusative: "в прошлый четверг", with the day in lower case.
7.10 «Пока что» — "Пока что" means "so far, for now". Russian has no perfect tense to pair it with, so the plain past does the work.
7.11 «никто не пожаловался» — "Никто не пожаловался" is the compulsory double negation again. "Пожаловаться" is perfective and takes "на" plus the accusative when the complaint has an object.
7.12 «и это уже похоже на победу» — "Похоже на" plus the accusative means "looks like". "Уже" here means "already" and adds the sense that this is more than expected.
8.1 «Я стараюсь» — "Стараться" is imperfective, an effort that goes on. It is followed by an infinitive with nothing in between.
8.2 «вести бюджет» — "Вести бюджет" is the fixed collocation, literally "to lead a budget". "Бюджет" is the accusative of an inanimate masculine noun, unchanged.
8.3 «но получается не очень» — "Получается" is an impersonal verb with "-ся": there is no subject at all, and the sentence means "it does not work out well". "Не очень" is a mild understatement.
8.4 «Проблема» — The subject in the nominative. Russian has no article, so nothing marks "проблема" as "the problem".
8.5 «не в крупных тратах» — "Не в крупных тратах" uses "в" plus the prepositional after the negation. The construction "не в чём-то, а в чём-то" is fixed and both halves must keep the same case.
8.6 «а в мелких» — "А" contrasts with the negated half and is not the same as "и" or "но". "В мелких" keeps the prepositional to match "в крупных тратах" — the noun is dropped but the case is not.
8.7 «Кофе» — The nominative as subject. "Кофе" is indeclinable, so it looks the same everywhere.
8.8 «и такси» — "Такси" is also indeclinable and neuter: it has no plural ending, and the verb after it comes from the pair "кофе и такси".
8.9 «набегают быстрее, чем кажется» — "Набегают" is a figurative use of "набегать", "to add up". After "чем" comes a full clause: "чем кажется", where "кажется" is impersonal.
8.10 «В этом месяце» — "В этом месяце" takes "в" plus the prepositional, like "в прошлом месяце".
8.11 «я записываю» — The imperfective present "записываю" covers a habit that is still going on. Russian has no perfect, so the month being unfinished is not marked by the verb.
8.12 «каждый платёж» — "Каждый платёж" is the accusative object, unchanged in form. "Каждый" always takes a singular noun.
9.1 «Я плохо сплю» — "Плохо" is an adverb and goes before the verb. "Сплю" is the first person of the irregular "спать".
9.2 «если смотрю в ноутбук» — In an "если" clause about a repeated condition Russian uses the present. "Смотреть в" plus the accusative is how Russian talks about staring into a screen; "смотреть на" would mean looking at it from outside.
9.3 «поздно вечером» — "Поздно вечером" combines an adverb with the instrumental of time. Neither part takes a preposition.
9.4 «Я пробовал» — "Пробовать" is imperfective, an attempt made more than once. Its perfective partner "попробовать" would point to one single try.
9.5 «оставлять его» — After "пробовал" the verb stays in the infinitive, and the imperfective "оставлять" says it was a repeated arrangement, not one act.
9.6 «на кухне» — "На кухне" takes "на", not "в" — one of the fixed choices Russian makes for certain places, alongside "на работе" and "на почте".
9.7 «Это работает» — "Это" refers back to the whole arrangement. "Работает" is used figuratively for "it works", exactly as in English.
9.8 «но только» — "Но только" restricts what follows. "Только" stands directly before the part it limits.
9.9 «когда я действительно так делаю» — "Действительно" means "really, in fact". "Так делаю" is literally "do it that way": "так" replaces the whole action.
9.10 «Привычки» — The nominative plural as the subject, with no article: "привычки" means habits in general.
9.11 «гораздо легче запланировать» — "Гораздо" intensifies a comparative, like English "much". "Легче" is the comparative of "лёгкий", formed with "-е" and a consonant change.
9.12 «чем соблюдать» — After "чем" the form matches what came before: two infinitives, "запланировать" and "соблюдать". Note that the first is perfective and the second imperfective, and that difference is deliberate: planning happens once, keeping goes on.
10.1 «Моя старая машина» — "Моя старая машина" is the subject in the nominative, with both the possessive and the adjective agreeing in the feminine.
10.2 «наконец» — "Наконец" means "finally" and takes no commas here.
10.3 «умерла в феврале» — "Умерла" is a perfective past agreeing with the feminine "машина". Using it about a car is a live metaphor in Russian, as in English. "В феврале" is "в" plus the prepositional.
10.4 «Вместо того чтобы покупать новую» — "Вместо того чтобы" is a fixed conjunction followed by an infinitive: "покупать новую". The imperfective says the buying is thought of as a process, not a single act.
10.5 «я решил» — "Решить" is perfective, a decision made once.
10.6 «попробовать электричку» — After "решил" comes the infinitive "попробовать". "Электричка" is a suburban commuter train specifically, not any train — that would be "поезд".
10.7 «Дорога» — "Дорога" here means the journey rather than the road. The subject in the nominative.
10.8 «занимает больше времени» — "Занимает" is imperfective present, for something that is regularly true. "Больше времени" puts the noun in the genitive after "больше", as quantity words always do.
10.9 «но в пути можно читать» — "В пути" is a fixed prepositional phrase, "on the way", from the noun "путь". "Можно" is impersonal and takes an infinitive with no subject at all.
10.10 «Не думаю» — "Не думаю" carries the negation in the main clause, exactly as English does. The subject "я" can be dropped here, and often is.
10.11 «что я вернусь» — "Вернусь" is a perfective future with the particle "-сь". No auxiliary word is needed for the future.
10.12 «к вождению» — "Вернуться к" plus the dative: "к вождению". "Вождение" is a verbal noun, and Russian uses it where English uses the gerund "driving".
